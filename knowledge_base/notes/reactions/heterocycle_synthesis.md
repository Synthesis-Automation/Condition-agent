### Reaction Type

Benzofuroxan condensation with 1,3,5-trihydroxybenzene (phloroglucinol) followed by reductive deoxygenation with sodium dithionite.

### Mechanism Overview

* Step 1 involves a formal [4+2] cycloaddition-type condensation between benzofuroxan and phloroglucinol, facilitated by base, to construct the phenazine dioxide core. The base (DIPEA) likely deprotonates the phloroglucinol and mediates the ring closure (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
* Step 2 is a reductive deoxygenation using sodium dithionite under strongly basic conditions to yield the dihydroxyphenazine product (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).

### Solvent Notes

* **Methanol/Water** — A 4:1 MeOH/H₂O mixture is effective for the initial condensation, providing a homogeneous reaction medium for the polar substrates and products (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
* **Phosphate Buffer (pH 7.0)** — A 100 mM phosphate buffer is used as a wash/resuspension medium for the crude intermediate to remove salts and impurities effectively without decomposing the product (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
* **DMF/Water for Recrystallization** — DMF is a suitable solvent for recrystallizing the polar intermediate (1,3-dihydroxyphenazine dioxide), with water as an antisolvent. This yields a microcrystalline powder suitable for PXRD analysis (Note 14, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
* **DMF/Acetone/Water for Final Product** — The final product is purified by dissolution in DMF, charcoal treatment, and precipitation by adding water to the filtrate, followed by acetone washes. This sequence effectively removes colored impurities (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

* **Sodium Dithionite** — The grade (technical, 88%) is acceptable, but the reagent's freshness is critical for the reductive step; old batches can lead to complete failure of the reaction (Note 16, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

* The method is specific for the synthesis of 1,3-dihydroxy-substituted phenazines via condensation of benzofuroxan with a 1,3,5-trihydroxybenzene (phloroglucinol). The scope for other substitution patterns on the phenazine core using this route is not explored in this procedure (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).

### Functional Group Tolerance

* ✓ **Tolerates:** Hydroxyl groups (phenolic), fused aromatic systems (phenazine core).
* The procedure involves strongly basic conditions (5 equiv KOH) and a reducing agent (Na₂S₂O₄), which would be incompatible with many reducible or base-sensitive functional groups (e.g., aldehydes, ketones, halogens susceptible to reduction, esters to saponification) (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).

### Critical Conditions

* **Atmosphere** — The reductive deoxygenation step with sodium dithionite requires an inert atmosphere (argon) during the initial reduction period to prevent oxidation of the reagent or intermediates. The reaction is then deliberately exposed to air to complete the workup (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
* **pH Control in Quench** — Precise pH control is necessary when quenching the basic reduction mixture. Acetic acid is added until pH 5.0 is reached, monitored with a pH probe, as the deep color of the solution prevents visual indicator use (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
* **Recrystallization of Intermediate** — Recrystallization of the intermediate 1,3-dihydroxyphenazine dioxide from DMF/water, while not strictly necessary for reaction success, is recommended for highest reproducibility and purity of the final product (Note 14, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).

### Side Reactions

* No specific side reactions are discussed, but the critical importance of using fresh sodium dithionite implies that decomposition products of old reagent can inhibit or divert the desired reduction (Note 16, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).

### Procedure Hints

* A deep green color forms immediately upon adding DIPEA and water to the benzofuroxan/phloroglucinol mixture in methanol, indicating reaction initiation (Fig. 1, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
* For the reduction, adding aqueous KOH to the slurried dioxide intermediate produces a homogeneous dark green solution, confirming dissolution and likely deprotonation (Fig. 4, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).

### Scale-up Notes

* No specific scale-up modifications are noted, but operations like resuspending crude solids in a sintered funnel (Fig. 2) or slow addition of water to DMF filtrates may require adjusted timing or equipment for larger batches (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).

### Analytical Notes

* The intermediate (1,3-dihydroxyphenazine dioxide) and final product (1,3-dihydroxyphenazine) are both high-melting (>250 °C) solids (Notes 13 & 21, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
* **Solid Form Awareness:** The final product crystallizes as a pentahydrate. Upon drying, at least two different anhydrous crystal forms can exist, which could affect physical properties and analyses like PXRD (Note 15, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
* The intermediate dioxide crystallizes as a solvate with DMF and water (Note 14, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips

* The yield of the intermediate (dioxide) is improved and made more reproducible by including a recrystallization (DMF/water) prior to the reduction step, raising the yield for that step from ~51% to 83% (Note 14, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
* Using a pH probe is essential for accurately quenching the basic reduction reaction due to the intense color of the solution (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
* For the final purification, stirring the DMF solution of the crude product with decolorizing charcoal for 24 hours is necessary to effectively remove colored impurities (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 45).
