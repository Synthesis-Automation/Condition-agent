### Reaction Type
Nickel-catalyzed asymmetric hydrogenation of oximes to chiral hydroxylamines.

************
### Mechanism Overview
The reaction proceeds via nickel-catalyzed asymmetric hydrogenation. The (S,S)-Ph-BPE ligand coordinates to nickel(II) acetate, which is reduced *in situ* by H₂ to form the active nickel-hydride species. This hydride stereoselectively adds to the C=N bond of the oxime. The role of acetic acid is crucial; it protonates the nitrogen atom after hydride addition, facilitating the release of the chiral hydroxylamine product. High enantioselectivity is imparted by the chiral, bidentate phosphine ligand which creates a well-defined chiral pocket around the metal center.

### Solvent Notes
- **2,2,2-Trifluoroethanol (TFE) is critical.** The strongly electron-withdrawing nature of TFE activates the C=N bond of the oxime towards hydride addition and is necessary for high catalyst activity and enantioselectivity in this system. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236)
- **The reaction requires a high solvent-to-substrate ratio.** A low concentration (~0.2 M) is used to prevent product inhibition, as high concentrations of the generated hydroxylamine product are detrimental to catalyst activity. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236; Note 19)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
- **Ligand purity and handling are crucial.** (S,S)-Ph-BPE is air-sensitive. Solvents and the catalyst mixture must be handled in an inert atmosphere (glovebox) to prevent oxidation of the phosphine ligand, which would deactivate the catalyst. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236; Note 18)
- **Solvent purity is critical for catalyst performance.** Both TFE and acetic acid must be freshly distilled and degassed prior to use. Checkers identified this as a key factor for successful reaction outcomes. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236; Notes 19, 20)
- **A Bronsted acid additive is essential.** Acetic acid (AcOH) serves a dual purpose: it likely protonates the oxime nitrogen, increasing its electrophilicity, and protonates the intermediate metal-amide after hydride addition, enabling product release and catalyst turnover. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236)

### Critical Conditions
- **Inert atmosphere is mandatory.** Oxygen must be rigorously excluded to prevent ligand oxidation. Catalyst and degassed solvents are assembled in a glovebox. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236)
- **High hydrogen pressure is required.** The reaction is run under 50 bar H₂ pressure, indicating the hydrogenation step may be challenging and requires specialized high-pressure equipment (autoclave). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236)
- **Moderate temperature and long reaction time.** The hydrogenation proceeds at 50 °C over 24 hours, suggesting the catalyst turnover is slow. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236)

### Side Reactions
- **Catalyst deactivation by product.** The generated hydroxylamine product can inhibit the catalyst at high concentrations, which is why the reaction is run at high dilution. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236; Note 19)
- **Ligand oxidation** is a primary side reaction if oxygen is not excluded, leading to complete catalyst failure. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236; Note 18)
- **Over-reduction** to the corresponding amine is not reported but is a potential concern with extended reaction times or different catalyst systems.

### Procedure Hints
- **Work under inert atmosphere.** All manipulations involving the catalyst (Ni salt and ligand) and the degassed solvents must be performed in a glovebox or using rigorous Schlenk techniques. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236)
- **Use freshly distilled, degassed solvents.** This is explicitly highlighted by the checkers as critical for performance. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236; Notes 19, 20)
- **Maintain low substrate concentration.** Do not concentrate the reaction mixture; use the specified high solvent volume to avoid product inhibition. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236)

### Scale-up Notes
- Scaling up would require a suitably rated high-pressure autoclave with efficient overhead stirring and temperature control.
- The need for high dilution (0.2 M) means solvent volume and waste generation become significant concerns on large scale.
- Heat transfer and safe handling of high-pressure hydrogen gas are primary scale-up considerations.

### Analytical Notes
- **TLC for oxime formation (Step A):** Use 5:1 petroleum ether/EtOAc. Starting ketone (Rf ~0.54), oxime product (Rf ~0.40). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236; Note 7)
- **TLC for hydroxylamine (Step B):** Use 2:1 petroleum ether/EtOAc. Starting oxime (Rf ~0.74), hydroxylamine product (Rf ~0.2). The product can also be visualized with KMnO₄ stain. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236; Note 22)
- **Chiral HPLC for ee determination:** OJ-H column, 95:5 n-hexane/i-PrOH, 1.0 mL/min. For the product, tᵣ (S) = 9.8 min, tᵣ (R) = 10.9 min. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236; Note 25)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
- **Low yield/activity:** The most likely cause is oxygen contamination leading to ligand oxidation. Ensure all glassware is oven-dried, solvents are freshly distilled and degassed, and operations are performed under inert atmosphere. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236)
- **Low yield/activity:** Using old or wet TFE/AcOH can severely impact the reaction. Always use freshly distilled, anhydrous, and degassed solvents. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236; Notes 19, 20)
- **Recrystallization of oxime:** Petroleum ether is an effective recrystallization solvent for the acetophenone oxime intermediate, providing pure material as white crystals. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 236; Note 13)
