### Reaction Type  
Copper-catalyzed Suzuki–Miyaura cross-coupling of unactivated alkyl halides with arylboronates  

### Solvent Notes  
• Use anhydrous, nonpolar solvents (toluene dried over MgSO₄) to minimize boronate hydrolysis and base quenching (Org. Synth. 2025)  
• Avoid protic or highly coordinating solvents (e.g. DMF, MeOH) that can deactivate Cu–ligand complexes or promote protodeboronation (Org. Synth. 2025)  

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes  
• Cu(I) salts with diimine ligands (CuBr·SMe₂ + bathophenanthroline) enable challenging sp³ C–X oxidative addition and suppress β-hydride elimination (Org. Synth. 2025)  
• Strong, hindered alkoxide bases (NaOtBu) are essential for efficient transmetalation; weaker bases give low conversion (Org. Synth. 2025)  
• Employ ≥1.5 equiv of base and ≥1.8 equiv of boronic ester to compensate for competitive homocoupling (Org. Synth. 2025)  

### Side Reactions  
• Homocoupling of arylboronates increases with insufficient base or ligand; suppress by excess boron reagent and optimized Cu/ligand ratio (Org. Synth. 2025)  
• β-Hydride elimination from alkyl–Cu intermediates can erode yields; minimized by proper ligand choice and controlled temperature (Org. Synth. 2025)  

### Substrate Scope and Limitations  
• Unactivated primary and secondary alkyl bromides can be coupled, but secondary substrates are more prone to β-hydride elimination (Org. Synth. 2025)  
• Arylboronic pinacol esters tolerate electron-donating and -withdrawing substituents; pinacol protection improves stability vs. free boronic acids (Org. Synth. 2025)  

### Critical Conditions  
• Conduct reaction in a sealed, oven-dried pressure vessel to maintain anhydrous conditions and allow heating to 80 °C for 24 h (Org. Synth. 2025)  
• Charge base, catalyst, ligand, and boronate under air but ensure solvent and glassware are rigorously dried (Org. Synth. 2025)  
• Monitor reaction color (black slurry indicates active catalyst species) and use TLC (Rf ~0.8 in PE) to track consumption (Org. Synth. 2025)  

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips  
• Typical isolated yields are 65–73%; small‐scale runs can give up to 78% yield (Org. Synth. 2025)  
• Glassware and stir bars must be oven‐dried to prevent moisture; incomplete drying leads to lower conversion (Org. Synth. 2025)  
• Filtration through silica/diatomite removes copper salts and base; multiple chromatography steps may be needed to separate homocoupling byproducts (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Solvent Notes  
• Good: CH2Cl2 — anhydrous dichloromethane dissolves Pd(OAc)2 and ligand for rapid precatalyst formation (Org. Synth.)  
• Good: tBuOH — protic high-bp solvent dissolves inorganic bases (K2CO3, K3PO4) in Suzuki/Hartwig couplings; use blast shield above 82 °C due to pressure buildup (Org. Synth.)  
• Et3N — dual role as base and co-solvent for catalyst activation; ensure dryness to prevent catalyst deactivation (Org. Synth.)  

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes  
• Pre-activate Pd(OAc)2 (1 mol%) with biaryl phosphine (CM-Phos, 4 mol%) in CH2Cl2/Et3N at 50 °C for 5 min, then remove volatiles to isolate active precatalyst (Org. Synth.)  
• For aryl mesylates, employ 1 mol% Pd and 4 mol% ligand to overcome the low reactivity of sulfonate leaving groups (Org. Synth.)  
• Use 2–3 equiv of arylboronic acid in Suzuki couplings of aryl mesylates to drive conversion and suppress homocoupling (Org. Synth.)  
• K2CO3 for Buchwald–Hartwig aminations, K3PO4 for Suzuki couplings—both bases are readily available and effective with tBuOH (Org. Synth.)  
• Dry and degas all solvents and reagents (e.g., distill tBuOH from Na, Et3N from KOH, CH2Cl2 from CaH2) to avoid catalyst poisoning (Org. Synth.)  

### Side Reactions  
• Incomplete mesylation if methanesulfonyl chloride ≤1.1 equiv—use ≥2 equiv to ensure full conversion in <3 h (Org. Synth.)  
• Poor stirring on scale leads to sticky, glutinous mixtures and lower yields—ensure vigorous agitation (Org. Synth.)  
• Sealed-vessel heating of tBuOH above its bp can cause dangerous overpressure—use blast shield and rated glassware (Org. Synth.)  

### Substrate Scope and Limitations  
• Aryl mesylates bearing electron-neutral or weakly donating groups (e.g., t-Bu) couple efficiently under these Pd/CM-Phos conditions (Org. Synth.)  
• Large-scale reactions require longer times (24 h) than small-scale examples to reach full conversion (Org. Synth.)  
• Sterically hindered or deactivated aryl sulfonates may need increased ligand loading or temperature (Org. Synth.)  

### Critical Conditions  
• Rigorously exclude air and moisture: evacuate/backfill flask three times with N2 prior to catalyst activation and substrate addition (Org. Synth.)  
• After precatalyst formation, strip volatiles at high vacuum (0.01 mmHg) to obtain a solid pre-catalyst, then add substrate/base/solvent (Org. Synth.)  
• Pre-stir at room temperature for 10 min before ramping to 120 °C for 24 h; this “settling” period improves reproducibility (Org. Synth.)  
• Efficient stirring prevents local overheating and ensures homogeneous temperature and mixing (Org. Synth.)  

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips  
• Rapid TLC monitoring of mesylate formation (Rf differentiation in EtOAc–DCM–hexanes) avoids over-reaction or degradation (Org. Synth.)  
• Crystallization of aryl mesylate from CH2Cl2/hexane at –14 °C yields analytically pure material and simplifies workup (Org. Synth.)  
• Pre-distillation or purification of phenylboronic acid enhances yield by minimizing proto-deboronation (Org. Synth.)  
• Dry crude product under high vacuum after evaporation to remove residual tBuOH or Et3N, improving downstream yields (Org. Synth.)

### Reaction Type
Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling catalyzed by nickel.

            
### Solvent Notes
*   **t-Amyl alcohol (2-methylbutan-2-ol)** is an effective "green" solvent for nickel-catalyzed Suzuki-Miyaura couplings. It can replace more traditional and/or hazardous solvents and is compatible with heterocyclic substrates. (Source document)
*   **2-Me-THF** is another viable "green" solvent for this nickel-catalyzed methodology, offering a suitable alternative to traditional ethereal solvents. (Source document)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
*   **NiCl₂(PCy₃)₂** is a highly effective pre-catalyst for the coupling of heterocyclic (e.g., pyrimidine, pyridine) halides and phenolic derivatives (e.g., sulfamates, carbamates) with aryl boronic acids. Catalyst loadings as low as 0.5-1 mol% can be used for certain substrate combinations. (Source document)
*   **Anhydrous K₃PO₄** is a suitable base for this nickel-catalyzed coupling. The use of anhydrous material can eliminate bubbling observed during flame-drying steps. (Note 2 & 3)
*   **Heterocyclic boronic acids**, such as 3-furanylboronic acid, are competent coupling partners under these conditions, enabling the synthesis of bis(heterocyclic) motifs. (Source document)

### Side Reactions
*   No specific side reactions are highlighted for suppression in this procedure. The high yields and clean reaction profiles described suggest the chosen conditions (NiCl₂(PCy₃)₂, t-Amyl alcohol, K₃PO₄) effectively suppress common Suzuki side reactions like protodeboronation and homocoupling for these substrate classes. (Source document)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
*   **Electrophile Scope:** The methodology is broadly applicable to **heterocyclic halides (Cl, Br, I)** and **phenol-derived electrophiles** (carbamates, sulfamates, mesylates, pivalates). This is a key advantage over some palladium systems which struggle with certain phenolic derivatives. (Source document)
*   **Heterocycle Tolerance:** A wide range of heterocycles in both coupling partners are tolerated, including **pyridine, pyrimidine, pyrazole, quinoline, isoquinoline, carbazole, and dihydrobenzofuran**. (Source document)
*   **Bis(heterocycle) Synthesis:** The method is particularly useful for constructing **bis(heterocyclic) frameworks**, which are often challenging to access via traditional palladium catalysis. (Source document)

### Critical Conditions
*   **Atmosphere:** The reaction is performed under an inert atmosphere (N₂ or Ar), with evacuation/backfilling cycles performed on the solid mixture before solvent addition. (Procedure)
*   **Catalyst Activation:** A **pre-stirring period at room temperature** (1 h in this case) is used after mixing all components in solvent before heating. This likely allows for catalyst activation or formation of the active species. (Procedure)
*   **Temperature:** Reactions are typically heated between **100–120 °C** for completion. (Procedure & Discussion)
*   **Stoichiometry:** The use of **excess boronic acid (2.5–4.0 equiv)** and **base (4.5–7.2 equiv)** is common, especially for more challenging substrates like phenol derivatives. (Table footnotes)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
*   **Workup:** Washing the organic extract with **1.0 M NaOH** is a critical purification step to remove boronic acid residues and other acidic byproducts. (Procedure)
*   **Purification:** For solid products, **hot filtration** of the recrystallization solution through pre-heated glassware can remove insoluble impurities, but it may not always be necessary if product purity is already high. If performed, the glassware must be hot to prevent premature product crystallization on the frit. (Note 7)
*   **Scale:** The procedure is scalable, with consistent yields observed at half-scale. (Note 8)
