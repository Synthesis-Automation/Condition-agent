### Reaction Type

The procedure describes a multi-step synthesis of a highly substituted fluorenyl-based ligand precursor. The key step is a Lewis acid-catalyzed Friedel-Crafts-type alkylation/cyclization.

### Mechanism Overview

* Step A (Methylene Bridge Installation): A fluorene is deprotonated by a strong base (KOt-Bu) to form an anion, which attacks paraformaldehyde in a nucleophilic addition, forming a hydroxymethyl intermediate that dehydrates to the exo-methylene product (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 350-366; *   Step D (Cyclization): Boron trichloride (BCI₃) acts as a strong Lewis acid to activate the benzylic chlorides in compound 3. This generates highly electrophilic carbocationic intermediates (or tight ion pairs) which undergo successive Friedel-Crafts alkylations with two equivalents of the nucleophilic exo-methylene fluorene 1, ultimately forming the central seven-membered ring (Org. ### Side Reactions

* **Grignard Quenching Hazard:** Quenching excess methylmagnesium bromide with aqueous NH₄Cl produces methane gas with vigorous bubbling. The quenching must be performed slowly and carefully to avoid foam-over (Note 15, Org. The procedure uses 2 equivalents of the nucleophilic arene to drive the double alkylation to completion and suppress these side paths (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 350-366;

### Scale-up Notes

* The exothermic reactions (Grignard addition, BCl₃ addition) would require more controlled cooling and slower addition rates on larger scale to manage heat release.
* The volume of gas (CH₄) generated during the Grignard quench scales with the amount of reagent, requiring a larger vessel headspace and even more cautious quenching.
* Chromatography on large scale (315 g silica) uses a short, wide column (8 cm diameter, 14 cm height) and large fraction sizes for efficient processing (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 350-366;
