### Reaction Type

Nickel-catalyzed photoredox C–N coupling (Buchwald–Hartwig type variant).

### Mechanism Overview

• The catalytic cycle proceeds via a Ni(I)/Ni(III) manifold, distinct from the thermal Ni(0)/Ni(II) pathway (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).
• Single-electron transfer (SET) from the photocatalyst to the Ni(II) resting state generates the active Ni(I) species.
• Oxidative addition is facilitated by the photoexcited state, bypassing the high activation barrier typical for sp3-hybridized electrophiles.

### Solvent Notes

✓ **Good:** DMA or DMSO — high polarity stabilizes the nickel intermediates and solubilizes inorganic bases (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).
✗ **Avoid:** THF — leads to inconsistent conversion due to poor solubility of the cesium base and potential radical abstraction from the solvent (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

• **Ligand:** dtbbpy (4,4'-di-tert-butyl-2,2'-bipyridine) is crucial; less hindered bipyridines result in significantly lower yields due to rapid catalyst decomposition (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).
• **Photocatalyst:** Iridium-based catalysts (e.g., Ir(dF(CF3)ppy)2(dtbbpy)PF6) outperform organic dyes in this specific transformation due to longer excited-state lifetimes (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

• **Electrophiles:** Primary and secondary alkyl bromides perform well; tertiary bromides undergo elimination preferentially (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).
• **Nucleophiles:** Hindered secondary amines require elevated temperature (35–40 °C) to achieve full conversion (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).

### Functional Group Tolerance

✓ **Tolerates:** Ester, ketone, nitrile, heterocycles (e.g., pyridine).
✗ **Incompatible:** Free carboxylic acids (protonate the base, inhibit catalysis) and nitro groups (quench the photoexcited state) (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).

### Critical Conditions

• **Light Source:** 450 nm LEDs are optimal; 400 nm leads to substrate decomposition, while >500 nm provides insufficient energy for SET (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).
• **Atmosphere:** Strict oxygen exclusion is required; oxygen quenches the photocatalyst and oxidizes the Ni(I) intermediate (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).

### Side Reactions

• **Reductive Dehalogenation:** The primary side product results from hydrogen atom transfer (HAT) to the alkyl radical intermediate; keeping the amine concentration high suppresses this pathway (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).

### Scale-up Notes

• On larger scales (>5 mmol), efficient light penetration becomes rate-limiting. Use a "slurry" setup or flow chemistry to maintain high surface-area-to-volume ratios (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).
• External cooling fans are required to offset LED heating and maintain 25–30 °C reaction temperature (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).

### Analytical Notes

• Monitor reaction by LCMS: look for mass of the amine starting material decreasing and product mass increasing; the alkyl bromide starting material often appears as a broad peak due to on-column decomposition (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).
• TLC is unreliable due to UV-inactive alkyl chains and nickel complex interference (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips

• If the reaction stalls, adding an additional 0.5 mol % of both the Ni catalyst and photocatalyst often restarts the cycle (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).
• Filter through a short pad of silica gel to remove nickel residues before concentration to simplify purification (Simulated Source, JACS 2025).
