### Reaction Type

Dual nickel/photoredox-catalyzed C-alkylation of nitroalkanes with alkyl iodides.

### Mechanism Overview

* The reaction proceeds via a Ni(I)/Ni(III) catalytic cycle coupled with photoredox catalysis to generate alkyl radicals. The key step involves single-electron reduction of an alkyl iodide by a photoexcited Ir(III)*catalyst to form an alkyl radical, which is then trapped by a Ni(0) complex to form a Ni(I)-alkyl species. This undergoes oxidative addition with the nitroalkane-derived nitronate to form a Ni(III) complex, which reductively eliminates to yield the C-alkylated product and regenerate the Ni(I) catalyst. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 64-85;*   The use of a strong, non-nucleophilic base (KOtBu) is critical for complete deprotonation of the nitroalkane to form the nitronate nucleophile. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 64-85;

### Solvent Notes

* **Dioxane** is used as the reaction solvent because it effectively dissolves the inorganic base (KOtBu) and is compatible with the photoredox/Ni catalytic system, allowing efficient light penetration. (Org. ### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

* **Nickel Catalyst:** NiBr₂·diglyme complex is preferred for its stability and solubility. It must be weighed and handled quickly as it is hygroscopic and can turn semi-solid upon exposure to air. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 64-85; ***Ligand:** Bathocuproine is used as the ligand for nickel. It forms a pink suspension when pre-mixed with NiBr₂·diglyme in dioxane. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 64-85;*   **Photoredox Catalyst:** A catalytic amount of Ir(ppy)₃ is sufficient. Its role is to absorb blue light and generate the alkyl radical from the iodide. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 64-85; *   **Base:** Potassium tert-butoxide (KOtBu) is used as a strong base to generate the potassium nitronate in situ. Its addition to dioxane forms a cloudy suspension. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 64-85;

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

* **Alkyl Halides:** Primary and secondary alkyl iodides are competent electrophiles in this transformation. The procedure demonstrates the use of a primary iodide derived from a phthalimide-protected alcohol. (Org.
