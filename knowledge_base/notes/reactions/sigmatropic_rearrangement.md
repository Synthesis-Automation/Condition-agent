### Reaction Type
[5,5]-Sigmatropic rearrangement (specifically, a silanol epoxide rearrangement).

### Mechanism Overview
*   The key transformation is a stereospecific oxonium-ene [5,5]-sigmatropic rearrangement, promoted by a trityl cation (Ph3C⁺) generated in situ from Ph3CBF₄. The trityl cation activates the silanol-epoxide substrate by forming an oxonium intermediate, which undergoes concerted, stereospecific bond reorganization to form a new C–C bond and the tetrahydrofuran product. The rate-limiting step is likely the formation or rearrangement of this activated oxonium species. Full procedure available in source file. (Sathyamoorthi, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217; 
### Solvent Notes
*   CH₂Cl₂ is used for the key rearrangement step. It effectively dissolves both the organic substrates and the ionic catalyst/salt. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   Toluene is used to dissolve the crude product from the silylation step prior to filtration and chromatography, likely to ensure complete solubility and prevent premature crystallization on silica. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
*   Trityl tetrafluoroborate (Ph3CBF₄) is used as a precursor to generate the trityl cation (Ph3C⁺) in situ, which is the Lewis acid catalyst for the rearrangement. It is moisture-sensitive and handled under nitrogen. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃) is included stoichiometrically with the catalyst (1 equiv relative to substrate) to scavenge any residual acidic byproducts (e.g., HBF₄) that could decompose the catalyst or product. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   Di-tert-butylsilyl bis(triflate) is a highly electrophilic silylating agent used with 2,6-lutidine as a hindered base to selectively mono-silylate a primary alcohol, forming a silanol. The steric bulk of the di-tert-butyl group is likely crucial for the subsequent rearrangement. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   m-Chloroperoxybenzoic acid (mCPBA) is used for the epoxidation of the alkene. The procedure uses the commercial 70-75% grade containing m-chlorobenzoic acid and water without purification. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
*   The method converts silanol-tethered epoxides to siloxy-tetrahydrofurans via a [5,5]-rearrangement. The stereochemistry of the starting epoxide is preserved in the product, indicating a stereospecific process. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   The substrate for rearrangement must contain both a silanol and an epoxide in the correct spatial arrangement (likely a 1,5-relationship) to participate in the concerted pericyclic event. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)

### Functional Group Tolerance
*   **✓ Tolerates:** Silyl ether (di-tert-butylsilanol), epoxide, alkene (prior to epoxidation).
*   **✗ Incompatible:** Strong acids or bases (can cleave the silyl ether or decompose the trityl catalyst). The silanol is sensitive to aqueous acid during workup. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)

### Critical Conditions
*   An inert atmosphere (N₂) is critical for all steps, especially the rearrangement catalyzed by the moisture-sensitive Ph3CBF₄. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   For the rearrangement, the reaction is initiated at 0 °C upon addition of Ph3CBF₄, then allowed to warm to room temperature over 2 hours. This controlled initiation may help manage exotherm or catalyst decomposition. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   The epoxidation with mCPBA is performed at 0 °C initially, then warmed to room temperature to complete the reaction, standard for controlling the exotherm of peracid reactions. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)

### Side Reactions
*   During the silylation step, a dimeric side-product (higher Rf than the desired silanol monomer) is observed by TLC. This is likely a disiloxane formed from condensation of two silanol molecules. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   Residual m-chlorobenzoic acid from the mCPBA oxidation must be removed by base washes (1 M NaOH) during workup. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   Excess peracid from the epoxidation must be quenched with aqueous sodium thiosulfate to avoid oxidizing other sensitive functional groups. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)

### Procedure Hints
*   The silyl triflate and base (2,6-lutidine) are added at 0 °C to control the exothermic reaction and potentially improve selectivity for the mono-silylated product over the dimer. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   Upon addition of Ph3CBF₄ to the rearrangement reaction mixture, a persistent bright yellow color develops, which is a visual indicator of catalyst activation (formation of the trityl cation). Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   Aqueous workup of the silanol product uses careful, portion-wise addition of saturated NaHCO₃ at 0 °C to quench excess silyl triflate and acid, minimizing hydrolysis of the acid-sensitive silanol product. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)

### Scale-up Notes
*   Syringe pumps are used for controlled addition of reagents (2,6-lutidine, alcohol) in the initial silylation step, which would be critical for managing exotherms and maintaining selectivity on larger scale. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
*   The epoxidation uses a portion-wise solid addition of mCPBA over 10 minutes at 0 °C, a standard scale-up practice to control gas evolution and heat generation from peracid decompositions. Full procedure available in source file. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 217)
