### Reaction Type

Hydrosilylation of alkynes (propiolate esters) with Lewis acid regiocontrol.

### Mechanism Overview

• Lewis acid (AlCl₃) activation of the silyl hydride (Et₃SiH) generates a silylium-like electrophile, which adds regioselectively to the electron-deficient alkyne (propiolate), forming the β-silyl acrylate (anti-Markovnikov product). The rate and regioselectivity are governed by this Lewis acid-mediated polar mechanism rather than metal-catalyzed pathways. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428;

### Solvent Notes

• Dichloromethane (CH₂Cl₂) — suitable for AlCl₃-mediated reactions, provides a non-coordinating environment that doesn't interfere with the Lewis acidic catalyst. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; prep procedure)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

• Anhydrous AlCl₃ (1.1 equiv) is the key Lewis acid catalyst; it must be handled under inert atmosphere due to moisture sensitivity. Lower purity (98.5%) performed equivalently to high-purity (99.999%). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; Note 2)
• Triethylsilane (Et₃SiH, 1.0 equiv) is the hydrosilylation reagent; it is added before the alkyne to pre-activate with AlCl₃. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; prep procedure)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

• The method is demonstrated for propiolate esters to give β-silyl acrylates. The discussion suggests vinyl silanes are valuable bioisosteres and intermediates, implying the method may extend to other electron-deficient alkynes. Limitations for internal alkynes or alkynes with coordinating groups are not specified. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; Discussion section)

### Functional Group Tolerance

✓ Tolerates: Ester (ethyl propiolate is the substrate).
✗ Incompatible: Not explicitly stated, but strong Lewis bases (amines, phosphines, etc.) would likely poison AlCl₃. Moisture is incompatible. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; prep procedure and hazard notes)

### Critical Conditions

• Atmosphere: Strict inert (N₂) conditions are required for handling AlCl₃ and Et₃SiH. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; Step 1A)
• Temperature: Reaction must be performed at 0 °C; higher temperatures may lead to side reactions or loss of regioselectivity. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; Step 1A)
• Addition order: Et₃SiH is added to the AlCl₃/CH₂Cl₂ mixture first, followed by dropwise addition of the alkyne. Reversing the order could lead to polymerization or unproductive pathways. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; Step 1A)

### Side Reactions

• Polymerization of the acrylate product or alkyne substrate is a potential side reaction if temperature is not controlled or if addition is too rapid. (Implied by procedure requiring controlled, dropwise addition at 0 °C) (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; Step 1A)

### Procedure Hints

• Quenching AlCl₃ requires very cautious, dropwise addition of saturated aqueous NaHCO₃ while the reaction is kept at 0 °C to control gas evolution and prevent exotherm. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; workup description)
• An emulsion can form during the aqueous workup; adding brine and allowing the mixture to sit helps break it. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; Note 10)
• The product is purified by short-path distillation. The distillate contains residual Et₃SiH, which is removed by extended heating under reduced pressure (40 °C, 0.6 Torr, 3-6 h). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; purification description and Note 14)

### Scale-up Notes

• The quench of AlCl₃ with aqueous NaHCO₃ is highly gas-evolution intensive. On scale, this must be done slowly with efficient cooling and stirring to manage the exotherm and CO₂ release. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; workup description)
• The distillation temperature is pressure-dependent (e.g., 85 °C at 0.630 Torr vs. 76 °C at 0.380 Torr). Vacuum strength must be monitored and adjusted for. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; Note 13 and 16)

### Analytical Notes

• Reaction progress can be monitored by TLC (SiO₂, 9:1 hexanes:EtOAc). Ethyl propiolate (SM) is UV-inactive with yellow stain (Rf 0.44), product is UV-active with yellow stain (Rf 0.59). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; Note 6 and Figure 4)
• Product purity is determined by qNMR using 1,3,5-trimethoxybenzene as an internal standard. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; Note 15)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips

• The distillation step to remove Et₃SiH can lead to some loss of product if heating under vacuum is excessive. The checkers found 3 h at 0.380 Torr was sufficient, while authors used 6 h at 0.630 Torr. Optimize this step based on vacuum strength. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 428; Note 14)
