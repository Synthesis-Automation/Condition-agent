### Reaction Type

NaBr/Selectfluor mediated alcohol oxidation.

### Mechanism Overview

* NaBr is oxidized by Selectfluor to generate reactive bromonium species (Br+) in situ, which then activates the alcohol as an intermediate hypobromite. Subsequent hydrolysis and elimination yields the carbonyl product. The reaction proceeds via a brominative pathway, avoiding stoichiometric metal oxidants. (Joshi, J. Org. Chem. 2023, 88, 11240; as cited in discussion)
* The use of an aqueous co-solvent is critical for the hydrolysis step of the proposed mechanism. (Joshi, J. Org. Chem. 2023, 88, 11240; as cited in discussion)

### Solvent Notes

* The reaction requires a **1:1 mixture of acetonitrile and water**. The organic solvent (MeCN) solubilizes the reagents and starting material, while water is essential for the hydrolysis step in the proposed mechanism. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
* The workup uses ethyl acetate for extraction, and product purification by flash chromatography uses **1% acetone in dichloromethane** as the eluent. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

* **Selectfluor** acts as both an oxidant and a fluorine source for generating the reactive brominating agent. It is used in 1.5 equivalents relative to the alcohol. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
* **Sodium bromide (NaBr)** is a cheap, stable source of bromide, used in 1.5 equivalents. It is oxidized in situ to generate the active oxidant. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

* This protocol is general for the oxidation of **secondary alcohols to ketones**, as demonstrated. (Joshi, J. Org. Chem. 2023, 88, 11240; as cited in discussion)
* The method is also reported to oxidize primary alcohols to carboxylic acids and aldehydes to carboxylic acids. (Joshi, J. Org. Chem. 2023, 88, 11240; as cited in discussion)
* The reaction enables oxidative lactonization of diols. (Joshi, J. Org. Chem. 2023, 88, 11240; as cited in discussion)
* The conditions can also be used for the bromination of electron-rich aromatic rings. (Joshi, J. Org. Chem. 2023, 88, 11240; as cited in discussion)

### Functional Group Tolerance

* The method is described as "functional-group tolerant" and is part of a "green" oxidation program aiming to avoid toxic metals like chromium and osmium. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100; discussion)
* Full procedure available in source file.

### Critical Conditions

* The reaction must be protected from light. The flask is stoppered, wrapped in aluminum foil, and stirred in a dark fume hood. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
* Reaction proceeds at ambient temperature (~24 °C) over 20 hours. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
* The reaction mixture turns orange immediately upon addition of Selectfluor, providing a visual indicator of reagent activation. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)

### Side Reactions

* No specific side reactions are detailed for this substrate. The bromination of electron-rich aromatics is noted as a separate, useful transformation under similar conditions, suggesting electron-rich systems could be susceptible to bromination as a side reaction during alcohol oxidation. (Joshi, J. Org. Chem. 2023, 88, 11240; as cited in discussion)

### Procedure Hints

* Addition order: NaBr is added to the alcohol in MeCN/H₂O, followed by Selectfluor. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
* Key observation: The initially cloudy mixture becomes a clear, colorless solution upon stirring with NaBr, prior to Selectfluor addition. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
* The workup requires quenching with **saturated aqueous sodium thiosulfate (Na₂S₂O₃)** to reduce any residual oxidizing species, which is visually confirmed by the disappearance of the orange color. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
* A subsequent wash with saturated aqueous sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃) is performed. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)

### Scale-up Notes

* The published procedure is already conducted on a multi-gram scale (3.0 g, 19.4 mmol of borneol). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
* Checkers successfully performed the reaction on half scale (9.7 mmol) with comparable yield (76% vs. 84%). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100; Note 19)

### Analytical Notes

* Reaction progress is monitored by TLC (silica gel, 10% EtOAc in hexanes), visualizing with KMnO₄ stain. For borneol/camphor: starting material Rf = 0.08, product Rf = 0.4. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
* The unpurified residue after workup and concentration is a waxy solid. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
* Product purity was confirmed by ¹H NMR and quantified by qNMR using p-iodotoluene as an internal standard. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100; Note 18)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips

* The yield for the oxidation of (-)-borneol to (-)-camphor is 84% after column chromatography. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
* Product isolation involves flash chromatography on silica gel (73 g) eluting with 1% acetone in CH₂Cl₂. The crude product is loaded in a mixture of toluene and CH₂Cl₂, with careful rinsing using the eluent to ensure quantitative transfer. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100; Note 17)
* Residual solvent is removed from the solid product using high vacuum (2x10⁻³ mbar) for 1.5 hours. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 100)
