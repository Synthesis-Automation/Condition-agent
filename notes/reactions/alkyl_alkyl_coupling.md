### Reaction Type

Suzuki–Miyaura cross-coupling, specifically a copper-catalyzed variant for alkyl–alkyl coupling.

### Mechanism Overview

The reaction proceeds via a Cu(I)/Cu(III) catalytic cycle. Oxidative addition of the alkyl halide to Cu(I) forms a Cu(III)-alkyl species. Transmetalation with the alkyl boronic ester (activated by base) forms a dialkyl-Cu(III) intermediate, which undergoes reductive elimination to form the C–C bond. The chelating N,N-ligand (TMEDA) stabilizes the copper catalyst and suppresses β-hydride elimination from the alkyl–Cu intermediates, which is a critical side pathway for sp³-halides. The use of NaOtBu as a strong base is essential for activating the alkyl boronic ester for transmetalation (Org. Synth. 2025).

### Solvent Notes

✓ Good: **Toluene** — Provides a non-polar, high-boiling medium suitable for the high temperatures (100–110 °C) required for transmetalation and reductive elimination of alkyl substrates. It also dissolves the alkyl boronic esters and catalyst components well (Org. Synth. 2025).
✗ Avoid: **Ethereal solvents (e.g., THF, dioxane)** — While common for aryl Suzuki couplings, they are less effective for alkyl–alkyl couplings under these conditions, potentially due to lower thermal stability or poorer solubility of the base/reagents (Org. Synth. 2025).

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

✓ **Catalyst:** Copper(I) iodide is the preferred pre-catalyst over other copper sources (e.g., CuTC, CuBr) for this transformation (Org. Synth. 2025).
✓ **Ligand:** TMEDA (N,N,N',N'-tetramethylethylenediamine) is critical. It acts as a chelating ligand to stabilize the active copper species and suppress β-hydride elimination, which is the major decomposition pathway for alkyl–copper intermediates (Org. Synth. 2025).
✓ **Base:** Sodium tert-butoxide (NaOtBu) is essential. Strong alkoxide bases are required to form the reactive "ate" complex from alkyl boronic esters for transmetalation (Org. Synth. 2025).
✗ **Alkyl Halide:** Secondary alkyl bromides and iodides work well. Primary alkyl chlorides are unreactive under these conditions. Alkyl mesylates or tosylates are not mentioned and are likely poor electrophiles for this Cu system (Org. Synth. 2025).

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

✓ **Works well:** Secondary alkyl bromides and iodides coupled with primary or secondary alkyl boronic esters (e.g., Bpin, Bneopentyl glycol). The method is particularly valuable for forming congested C(sp³)–C(sp³) bonds (Org. Synth. 2025).
⚠ **Limited:** Primary alkyl halides can suffer from competitive β-hydride elimination, especially if they are β-branched. Unactivated alkyl chlorides are generally unreactive (Org. Synth. 2025).
⚠ **Problematic:** Sterically hindered tertiary alkyl halides and boronic esters are not viable substrates due to steric constraints on transmetalation and reductive elimination (Org. Synth. 2025).

### Functional Group Tolerance

✓ **Tolerates:** Ethers (including THP-protected alcohols), silyl ethers (TBS), esters, ketones, nitriles, and alkenes (remote from the reaction site) (Org. Synth. 2025).
✗ **Incompatible:** Free alcohols, carboxylic acids, or primary amines — these protic functional groups likely interfere with the strong base (NaOtBu) and/or coordinate to the copper catalyst, poisoning it (Org. Synth. 2025).
⚠ **Caution with:** Base-sensitive groups (e.g., epoxides) or reducible groups (e.g., nitro) are not reported and may not survive the conditions (Org. Synth. 2025).

### Critical Conditions

• **Temperature:** High temperature (100–110 °C) is mandatory to achieve reasonable rates for the transmetalation and reductive elimination steps involving alkyl groups (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **Atmosphere:** An inert atmosphere (argon or nitrogen) is crucial to prevent oxidation of the Cu(I) catalyst and degradation of the alkyl boronic ester (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **Order of Addition:** The base (NaOtBu) should be added last, just before sealing and heating the pressure vessel, to minimize premature decomposition of reagents (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **Concentration:** Relatively high concentration (~0.5 M) is used to favor the bimolecular coupling steps (Org. Synth. 2025).

### Side Reactions

• **β-Hydride Elimination:** The major side reaction for alkyl–copper intermediates, leading to alkene byproducts. This is suppressed by using the chelating TMEDA ligand (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **Protodeboronation:** Decomposition of the alkyl boronic ester can occur, especially if exposed to moisture or protic impurities in the presence of strong base (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **Homocoupling:** Minor homocoupling of the alkyl halide can occur via radical pathways, but this is minimized under the optimized conditions (Org. Synth. 2025).

### Procedure Hints

• Conduct all operations under an inert atmosphere (glovebox or Schlenk line) (Org. Synth. 2025).
• Use a pressure vessel (sealable tube or microwave vial) to safely contain the solvent at 110 °C (Org. Synth. 2025).
• Add solid NaOtBu as the final step just before sealing the vessel to minimize exposure to atmospheric moisture (Org. Synth. 2025).
• Reaction times are typically 20–24 hours at 110 °C (Org. Synth. 2025).

### Scale-up Notes

• **Heat Transfer:** The exotherm from dissolving NaOtBu in the reaction mixture must be considered. For larger batches, controlled addition of the base or external cooling during addition may be necessary (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **Mixing:** Efficient stirring is critical to suspend/solubilize the inorganic base and ensure good contact between all phases (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **Pressure:** The use of a proper pressure-rated reactor (e.g., Parr vessel) is mandatory for safe scale-up due to the volatile solvent at high temperature (Org. Synth. 2025).

### Analytical Notes

• **TLC:** Useful for monitoring consumption of the alkyl halide starting material (stains with KMnO₄ or phosphomolybdic acid). The product often has a slightly higher Rf than the halide starting material (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **NMR:** The disappearance of signals from the halide starting material and the appearance of new, often complex multiplet signals for the coupled product are key indicators. ¹¹B NMR can monitor boronic ester consumption (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **GC/MS or LC/MS:** Can be used to detect the desired product mass and identify volatile side products like alkenes from β-hydride elimination (Org. Synth. 2025).

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips

• **Low Yield / No Reaction:** Ensure complete exclusion of oxygen and moisture. Check the quality of NaOtBu—it must be free-flowing and white; yellow or clumped base is deactivated. Confirm the reaction is reaching the correct internal temperature (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **Alkene Byproduct Formation (β-Hydride Elimination):** This indicates ligand dissociation or degradation. Ensure a fresh, high-quality source of TMEDA is used. Consider slightly increasing the ligand loading (1.1–1.2 equiv relative to CuI) (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **Protodeboronation:** This suggests wet reagents or solvent. Rigorously dry toluene and ensure boronic ester is of high purity. Adding molecular sieves (3Å or 4Å) to the reaction mixture can help (Org. Synth. 2025).
• **Incomplete Conversion of Halide:** Extend reaction time (up to 36–48 hours) or increase temperature slightly (up to 120 °C) if the pressure vessel permits (Org. Synth. 2025).

### Reaction Type

Copper-catalyzed cross-coupling of alkyl halides with alkylboron reagents. This is a variant of metal-catalyzed sp³–sp³ coupling.

### Mechanism Overview

Mechanism involves transmetalation of the alkylboron species to the Cu(I) catalyst, followed by halide abstraction from the alkyl electrophile to form the new C–C bond. The use of NaOtBu is critical, likely serving as both a base to activate the boronate complex and to generate a reactive copper-alkoxide intermediate for transmetalation. The ligand (neocuproine) stabilizes the copper center and facilitates the key steps. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Solvent Notes

✓ **Toluene** — Effective for this copper-catalyzed coupling, providing good solubility for reactants and catalyst while being compatible with the high temperature (80°C). (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ Avoid protic or highly polar solvents (e.g., DMF, alcohols, water) — They can interfere with the transmetalation step or promote protodeboronation/side reactions of the alkylboron reagent. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

✓ **Catalyst:** Copper(I) iodide is a suitable, air-stable Cu(I) source. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ **Ligand:** Neocuproine is essential for successful coupling; bidentate nitrogen ligands are optimal for stabilizing the copper center and promoting the catalytic cycle. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ **Base:** Sodium tert-butoxide (NaOtBu) is required; other bases (e.g., K₃PO₄, Cs₂CO₃) gave significantly lower yields. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ **Alkylboron reagent:** Alkyltriisopropoxyborates (generated in situ from alkylboronic acids and NaOtBu/i-PrOH) are effective coupling partners. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

✓ **Works well for primary alkyl bromides and iodides.** Secondary alkyl halides are not reported and may be challenging due to competing elimination or slower oxidative addition. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ **Primary alkylboronic acids** are effective coupling partners when converted to the corresponding triisopropoxyborate. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ Limitations: The method is not demonstrated with aryl, vinyl, or heteroaryl halides/boron reagents. Halides prone to elimination (e.g., β-branched) may not be suitable. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Functional Group Tolerance

✓ **Tolerates:** Ethers (including THF-derived substrates), silyl-protected alcohols. (Org. Synth. 2025)
⚠ **Conditional:** Boronic esters (from diols) can be used but require in situ hydrolysis to the boronic acid. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ **Incompatible:** The presence of free, protic functional groups (acids, alcohols) is likely incompatible with the strongly basic NaOtBu conditions. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Critical Conditions

* **Temperature:** 80°C is optimal. Lower temperatures slow the reaction; higher temperatures may promote decomposition. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **Atmosphere:** Reactions are performed under nitrogen to prevent oxidation of the Cu(I) catalyst and the sensitive alkylboronate species. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **Order of addition:** The alkylboronic acid is first converted to the triisopropoxyborate salt with NaOtBu in i-PrOH before adding catalyst, ligand, halide, and solvent. This pre-formation step is crucial. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Side Reactions

* **Protodeboronation:** The alkylboron species can decompose via this pathway, especially if not properly formed into the borate salt or if protic impurities are present. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **β-Hydride elimination:** A potential side reaction from the alkyl-copper intermediate, especially with secondary halides. The use of primary substrates and the stabilizing neocuproine ligand mitigate this. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **Homocoupling** of the alkyl halide (Wurtz-type) is a possible competitive reaction. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Procedure Hints

* Pre-form the alkyltriisopropoxyborate salt by stirring the alkylboronic acid with 3 equiv NaOtBu in i-PrOH for 30 min at room temperature under N₂ before adding other components. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* Maintain a strict nitrogen atmosphere throughout catalyst/ligand charging and reaction heating to preserve the Cu(I) oxidation state. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* The reaction mixture typically turns dark brown/black upon heating, which is normal. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Scale-up Notes

* The exotherm during the initial borate formation (acid + base) should be controlled on scale. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* Concentration (0.25 M) should be maintained to ensure good mixing and heat transfer. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* Efficient nitrogen sparging/deoxygenation will be more critical on larger scale to protect the catalyst system. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Analytical Notes

* Reaction progress can be monitored by TLC (SiO₂) or GC/MS for volatile products. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* The product alkane is typically less polar than the starting alkyl halide on SiO₂ TLC. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* For non-volatile products, quenching an aliquot and analyzing by ¹H NMR is effective. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips

* **Low yield?** Ensure the boronic acid is fresh and high purity. Check the nitrogen atmosphere integrity and that the NaOtBu is free-flowing and not clumped (indicating moisture exposure). (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **No reaction?** Verify the pre-formation of the borate salt (should form a homogeneous mixture/suspension in i-PrOH). Consider increasing catalyst/ligand loading slightly if substrate is particularly unreactive. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **Product purity:** The workup includes a brine wash to help remove copper salts and inorganic bases. A final silica gel column chromatography is typically necessary to obtain pure product. (Org. Synth. 2025)
