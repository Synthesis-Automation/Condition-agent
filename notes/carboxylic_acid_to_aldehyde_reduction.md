### Reaction Type  

Reduction of aromatic carboxylic acids to aldehydes via pinacolborane activation (DMAP/Tf-DMAP)  

### Solvent Notes  

• MeCN is preferred over DCM or ethers: it dissolves Tf-DMAP, supports 60 °C operation, and avoids exotherm/runaway risks (Shen et al.).  
• Avoid low-bp chlorinated solvents (DCM): narrow thermal window and poor Tf-DMAP solubility hinder scale-up.  
• Other solvents (THF, toluene, DCE, dioxane, DME) gave slurries or lower yields even at elevated T; clarity of the acyl-DMAP solution correlates with reproducibility.  

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes  

• Use Tf-DMAP (1.2 equiv) to generate mixed anhydride; follow with DMAP (total ∼2.1 equiv) to form acyl-pyridinium intermediate.  
• Premix DMAP and HBpin (DMAP·HBpin complex) before addition to ensure uniform activation and mitigate local over-reduction.  
• Slight deficiency of HBpin (∼1.0 equiv) avoids over-reduction to alcohol while ensuring complete aldehyde formation.  

### Side Reactions  

• Over-reduction to the corresponding alcohol occurs when HBpin > 1.05 equiv or when reductant is added neat into unconverted acid mix.  
• Amide (RCO–NH₂) and nitrile byproducts arise from quenching residual acyl-DMAP with NH₄Cl; ammonia traps the acyl intermediate (Shen et al.).  
  – Suppress by avoiding strong ammoniacal quench; use mild aqueous workup or buffered salt that does not generate free NH₃.  

### Substrate Scope and Limitations  

• Aromatic carboxylic acids convert cleanly to aldehydes; strong UV chromophores (e.g., naphthoic acid) aid in-process monitoring.  
• Aliphatic acids may require re-optimization of solvent or temperature due to differing solubility and reactivity of acyl-DMAP species.  
• Electron-poor or ortho-substituted acids could slow formation of the mixed anhydride; raise the first-stage T or prolong time.  

### Critical Conditions  

• Two-stage addition:  

  1. Stage 1 (acyl-DMAP formation): Tf-DMAP + DMAP + CA in MeCN at 60 °C for 2–3 h gives full conversion to acyl-pyridinium.  
  2. Stage 2 (reduction): add premixed DMAP·HBpin/MeCN slowly at 60 °C to control exotherm and minimize byproducts.  
• Reverse addition (adding acyl-DMAP solution into HBpin) leads to poor selectivity, unreacted acid, and over-reduction (Shen et al.).  
• Maintain careful temperature control during reductant addition (∆T < 10 °C) to avoid runaway and spraying.  

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips  

• If yield is low, check completeness of Stage 1 by TLC or UV–Vis (acyl-DMAP absorbs); prolong or raise T before adding HBpin.  
• If significant alcohol forms, reduce HBpin slightly (<1.0 equiv) or slow addition rate.  
• If nitrile/amide byproducts appear, revise aqueous quench (avoid NH₄Cl); consider NaHCO₃ wash or dilute HCl to neutralize DMAP·TfOH.
