This is a preparation of S-trifluoromethyl trifluoromethanesulfonothioate (TTST), a versatile electrophilic trifluoromethylthiolating agent.

### Mechanism Overview
*   The proposed mechanism involves the initial generation of triflyl chloride intermediate (CF₃SO₂Cl) from the reaction of sodium triflinate (Langlois reagent) with triflic anhydride. This then disproportionates/condenses to form the target TTST (CF₃SO₂SCF₃) with the elimination of SO₂ and the precipitation of sodium triflate. The reaction is exothermic and proceeds under thermal conditions. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Scheme 1)
*   TTST itself is a source of electrophilic CF₃S⁺ equivalents, but it can also be converted to nucleophilic CF₃S⁻ and radical species (CF₃S• and CF₃•) under appropriate conditions, making it a highly versatile and atom-economical reagent. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Discussion)

### Solvent Notes
*   **Chlorobenzene** — Used as the reaction solvent. It is high-boiling (bp ~132 °C), which is necessary for the distillation of the product (bp ~62-67 °C) directly from the reaction mixture. It must be dried (e.g., kept over 3Å molecular sieves) to prevent hydrolysis of reagents. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Notes 6, 14)
*   **Ethyl acetate** — Used for pre-purification of commercial sodium trifluoromethanesulfinate via a washing/filtration process to remove impurities. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Note 2)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
*   **Sodium trifluoromethanesulfinate (Langlois reagent)** — Must be of high purity and finely powdered to ensure complete reaction. It is hygroscopic and requires rigorous drying (e.g., heating under high vacuum) unless handled in an inert atmosphere glovebox. Particle size and dryness critically impact reaction efficiency and yield. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Notes 2, 4, 5)
*   **Triflic anhydride** — Used in 1.4 equivalents relative to sodium triflinate. Using less leads to unreacted starting material; using more leads to unreacted triflic anhydride. This slight excess is optimal for complete conversion. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Note 8)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
*Full procedure available in source file.*
*   TTST is a general reagent for synthesizing other electrophilic trifluoromethylthiolating agents (e.g., N-, O-, S-based reagents) in one step from appropriate nucleophiles. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Scheme 2 left)
*   As an electrophilic reagent, it reacts with electron-rich (hetero)aromatics, carbanions (e.g., from active methylene compounds), and alkenes (with LiBr as an additive) to introduce the SCF₃ group. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Scheme 2 right)

### Functional Group Tolerance

*   **TTST itself** is thermally stable (no decomposition <1% after 15h at 130°C in toluene-d₈), but it is a strong electrophile and will react with nucleophiles. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Note 18)

### Critical Conditions
*   **Atmosphere & Drying:** Extreme care must be taken to exclude moisture. All glassware must be flame-dried, and reagents must be rigorously dried. The sodium triflinate is dried in the reaction flask under vacuum at 80°C before use unless prepared in a glovebox. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Procedure, Notes 1, 5)
*   **Temperature Control:** The reaction is exothermic. The internal temperature must be controlled and kept around 75°C during the addition of triflic anhydride to prevent it from exceeding ~80°C. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Note 9)
*   **Mixing:** A **mechanical overhead stirrer** is essential because the reaction mixture becomes extremely viscous as sodium triflate precipitates. Magnetic stirring is insufficient. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Procedure, Note 10)

### Side Reactions
*   The formation of toxic **bis(trifluoromethyl) disulfide (CF₃SSCF₃, bp 34.6 °C)** is detected in the first part of the crude distillate. Care must be taken during distillation as this compound has high toxicity (LC50 200 ppm/min). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Note 14)

### Procedure Hints
*   **Addition Order:** Triflic anhydride is added dropwise to a heated suspension of dry sodium triflinate in dry chlorobenzene. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Procedure)
*   **Key Observation:** The reaction mixture becomes very viscous as it proceeds, confirming the formation of solid sodium triflate. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Procedure, Figure 1B)
*   **Isolation:** The product is isolated directly from the reaction mixture by **distillation** (bp 90°C collected at an oil bath temp up to 170°C). The solid sodium triflate byproduct remains in the pot and can be filtered in near-quantitative yield. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Procedure)
*   **Purification:** The crude distillate contains chlorobenzene solvent as the main impurity. High-purity TTST (>95%) is obtained via **fractional distillation** using a column packed with glass beads. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Procedure, Notes 15, 17)

### Scale-up Notes
*   The procedure is described on a 50g scale of sodium triflinate. Scale-up will amplify the challenges of **heat management** during the exothermic triflic anhydride addition and **efficient mixing** of the highly viscous slurry. Use of an appropriately powerful mechanical stirrer and careful temperature control is paramount. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Procedure, Notes 9, 10)
*   Distillation of the low-boiling product (and the toxic disulfide byproduct) on larger scale requires appropriate condenser cooling and receiver chilling, and must be conducted in a well-ventilated fume hood. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Procedure, Notes 12, 13, 14)

### Analytical Notes
*   **Purity Assessment:** The purity of TTST is determined by **quantitative ¹⁹F NMR** using an internal standard (e.g., 4-chlorobenzotrifluoride). This is a reliable method for fluorinated compounds. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Notes 15, 17)
*   **NMR Data:** TTST exhibits two characteristic quartets in the ¹⁹F NMR (δ -36.58 and -77.25, J = 5.2 Hz) and corresponding quartets in the ¹³C NMR. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Note 19)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
*   The key to obtaining a good yield is the use of **dry, fine-powdered sodium trifluoromethanesulfinate**. If the commercial material is not sufficiently pure or finely divided, a pre-purification via an ethyl acetate wash and regrinding is necessary. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Notes 2, 4)
*   The first fractional distillation gives material of ~85% purity suitable for most downstream reactions. A second distillation is required to achieve >95% purity. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Notes 16, 17)
*   **Safety First:** A thorough hazard and risk assessment is required before attempting this procedure. Key hazards include the toxicity of the disulfide byproduct, the corrosive and moisture-sensitive nature of triflic anhydride, and the use of chlorobenzene. All work must be done in a well-ventilated fume hood. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 562-581; Caution Note, Note 1)
