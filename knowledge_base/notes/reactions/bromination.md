### Reaction Type
Umpolung α-functionalization of amides via N‑hydroxy‑N‑methyl hydroxamate activation, specifically α‑bromination of unactivated secondary amides.


### Mechanism Overview
- Activation proceeds via formation of an N‑methyl‑N‑hydroxy hydroxamate (Weinreb-type intermediate), which is O‑tosylated to generate a good leaving group. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- The key step is a Lewis acid (MgBr₂)-mediated ring closure to form an N‑acyl nitrenium ion intermediate, which facilitates α‑deprotonation to generate an enolate equivalent. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- The nucleophilic enolate equivalent then attacks an electrophilic bromine source generated in situ (likely a bromo‑magnesium species), effecting an umpolung α‑bromination. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)

### Solvent Notes
- **Dichloromethane (DCM)** — Suitable for acid chloride formation and subsequent hydroxamate/tosylate synthesis steps under Schotten-Baumann conditions. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- **1,2‑Dichloroethane (DCE)** — Preferred over DCM for the final bromination step, likely due to its higher boiling point (83°C vs 40°C) which allows for a 40°C reaction temperature and better solvation of the MgBr₂/DIPEA system. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
- **Oxalyl chloride with catalytic DMF** — Standard protocol for efficient conversion of carboxylic acids to acid chlorides. The use of an empty balloon to equalize pressure during gas evolution (CO/CO₂) is noted. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- **MgBr₂·Et₂O** — Must be used in powdered form for effective reactivity. Solid blocks require crushing (e.g., via sonication) to ensure a homogeneous suspension and proper Lewis acid activation. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- **N,N‑Diisopropylethylamine (DIPEA)** — Used as a non‑nucleophilic base to deprotonate the α‑position in the final bromination step. Addition order (base added after MgBr₂ is suspended) is critical. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
- **Works for:** Unactivated aliphatic secondary amides, converted to their N‑hydroxy‑N‑methyl hydroxamate derivatives. The procedure demonstrated on a 4‑phenylbutanamide derivative. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- **Limitations:** Scope is defined by the initial hydroxamate synthesis and stability of the N‑acyl nitrenium intermediate. Likely incompatible with substrates bearing very acidic α‑protons or sensitive functional groups. Full procedure available in source file.

### Functional Group Tolerance
- ✓ **Tolerates:** Carboxylic acid (converted to acid chloride), ester (implied stability), aromatic rings. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- ✗ **Potential Incompatibility:** Functional groups sensitive to oxalyl chloride (alcohols, amines), strong Lewis acids (MgBr₂), or tosylating conditions. Free amines would interfere. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)

### Critical Conditions
- **Anhydrous conditions** are essential for the acid chloride formation and the final MgBr₂‑mediated bromination step (performed in a glovebox). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- **Temperature:** The final bromination requires heating to 40°C in DCE for 4 hours for complete conversion. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- **Order of addition:** For the final step, DIPEA is added dropwise to a pre‑suspended mixture of substrate and powdered MgBr₂·Et₂O in DCE. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)

### Side Reactions
- Hydrolysis of the acid chloride or the activated tosylhydroxamate intermediate is a potential side reaction if moisture is introduced. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- Over‑bromination or poly‑halogenation is a risk if the enolate equivalent is overly stabilized or if stoichiometry is not controlled. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)

### Procedure Hints
- **Acid Chloride Formation:** Immediate gas evolution (CO/CO₂) upon oxalyl chloride addition confirms reaction initiation. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- **Hydroxamate Formation:** Use of solid NaHCO₃ in portions under biphasic (Schotten-Baumann) conditions controls exotherm and neutralizes HCl. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- **Final Bromination:** Sonication of the reaction mixture containing MgBr₂·Et₂O solid blocks is necessary to crush them into a fine powder for effective reaction. A color change from white to pale yellow upon DIPEA addition is noted. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)

### Scale-up Notes
- Gas evolution during acid chloride formation requires proper venting or pressure equalization (e.g., an empty balloon). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- The exotherm during NaHCO₃ addition for hydroxamate formation should be managed by portion-wise addition over 5 minutes. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- Efficient mixing is critical when suspending powdered MgBr₂·Et₂O to avoid heterogeneous "dead zones". (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)

### Analytical Notes
- **TLC for Intermediate 3:** Rf = 0.37 (Hexane:Ethyl Acetate = 1:1, v/v). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- **TLC for Bromination:** Required to monitor consumption of tosylhydroxamate starting material 4; specific conditions not detailed in excerpt. Full procedure available in source file.
- **Product Purity:** Final α‑bromoamide purity (99%) was determined by quantitative ¹H NMR using 1,3,5‑trimethoxybenzene as an internal standard. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
- High yields (>85% over 3-4 steps) are achievable with strict adherence to anhydrous conditions, proper MgBr₂ particle size reduction, and monitored reaction times. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- If the final bromination is sluggish, ensure MgBr₂·Et₂O is a fine powder and that the reaction temperature is maintained at 40°C. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
- Crude intermediates (acid chloride 2, hydroxamate 3) can be carried forward without purification, but the tosylhydroxamate 4 requires chromatography before the key bromination step. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 601-624)
