### Reaction Type
Kumada–Tamao–Corriu cross-coupling of aryl/alkenyl Grignard reagents with aryl/alkenyl chlorides, bromides, or iodides.

### Mechanism Overview
- The Ni(0) catalyst (generated in situ) undergoes oxidative addition with the aryl halide (rate-limiting step for aryl chlorides).
- Transmetalation with the Grignard reagent follows, then reductive elimination yields the coupled product and regenerates the catalyst.
- The 1,3-bis(diphenylphosphino)propane (dppp) ligand is optimal, as its bite angle stabilizes the Ni(II) intermediate and facilitates reductive elimination (Org. Synth. 1978).

### Solvent Notes
✓ Good: THF or Et₂O — standard solvents for Grignard reagent stability and reactivity.
✗ Avoid: Protic or highly coordinating solvents — will quench the Grignard reagent (Org. Synth. 1978).

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
- Catalyst: Ni(II) salts (e.g., Ni(acac)₂) are reduced in situ to active Ni(0) by the Grignard reagent.
- Ligand: 1,3-Bis(diphenylphosphino)propane (dppp) is superior to other diphosphines (like dppe or dppb) for this coupling, providing higher yields and rates (Org. Synth. 1978).
- Grignard Reagent: Must be prepared and titrated accurately for consistent results.

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
- **Works Well:** Aryl chlorides, bromides, and iodides coupled with aryl or alkenyl Grignard reagents. Electron-rich and electron-deficient arenes are tolerated.
- **Problematic:** Substrates with β-hydrogens (e.g., alkyl Grignards) are not discussed and are prone to side reactions like β-hydride elimination.
- **Helps:** Using the more reactive Grignard reagent as the limiting agent can minimize homocoupling of the aryl halide (Org. Synth. 1978).

### Functional Group Tolerance
✓ Tolerates: Aromatic rings, alkenes (as coupling partners).
✗ Incompatible: Protic functional groups (alcohols, amines, acids), carbonyl groups (aldehydes, ketones, esters) — will react with the Grignard reagent (Org. Synth. 1978).

### Critical Conditions
- **Temperature:** Reaction is conducted at room temperature or with gentle warming (35°C), as excessive heat can promote side reactions.
- **Atmosphere:** Inert atmosphere (N₂ or Ar) is essential to prevent oxidation of the Grignard reagent and Ni(0) catalyst.
- **Addition Order:** The Grignard reagent is added slowly to a mixture of the aryl halide and catalyst to maintain low concentration of the nucleophile and suppress homocoupling (Org. Synth. 1978).

### Side Reactions
- **Homocoupling (Symmetrical Biaryl Formation):** Can occur via oxidative coupling of Grignard reagents. Suppressed by using an excess of the aryl halide and controlled addition of the Grignard.
- **Grignard Reagent Degradation:** Can occur via reaction with trace water or oxygen, emphasizing the need for dry, anaerobic conditions (Org. Synth. 1978).

### Procedure Hints
- Assemble all components under an inert atmosphere (N₂/Ar).
- Add the Grignard reagent solution dropwise via syringe to the stirred mixture of aryl halide and catalyst.
- Monitor by TLC or GC for consumption of the aryl halide starting material (Org. Synth. 1978).

### Scale-up Notes
- Maintain the same slow, controlled addition rate of the Grignard reagent to manage exotherm and minimize local high concentrations.
- Ensure efficient stirring and temperature control during addition, as the reaction mixture may become viscous (Org. Synth. 1978).

### Analytical Notes
- Reaction progress is monitored by TLC or GC for the disappearance of the aryl halide.
- The product biaryls/alkenyl-arenes are typically less polar than starting materials on silica gel TLC (Org. Synth. 1978).

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
- Accurate titration of the Grignard reagent is critical for reproducible yields.
- If homocoupling of the Grignard reagent is observed, use a more dilute solution and even slower addition.
- Low yields may indicate catalyst deactivation; ensure rigorous exclusion of oxygen and use freshly prepared catalyst (Org. Synth. 1978).
