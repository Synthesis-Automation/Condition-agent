### Reaction Type
Photoredox nickel-catalyzed C(sp2)–S cross-coupling.

### Mechanism Overview
• Key steps: Photocatalyst (4CzIPN) excitation generates a strong reductant via reductive quenching to reduce Ni(II) pre-catalyst to Ni(I/0). Oxidative addition of aryl bromide forms Ni(II)-aryl species, which undergoes transmetalation with thiolate (from thiol). Reductive elimination yields the thioether product and regenerates Ni(I/0) for the cycle. Rate-limiting step is likely oxidative addition or reductive elimination influenced by catalyst loading and light intensity. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • The reaction uses "adaptive dynamic homogeneous catalysis" where the catalyst system self-adjusts, but the key is the photoredox cycle generating the active nickel species. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; 
### Solvent Notes
• **N,N-Dimethylacetamide (DMA)** is the solvent of choice for this photoredox Ni-catalyzed C–S coupling. It solubilizes all components and is compatible with the photocatalytic cycle. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; 
### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
• Catalyst system: **NiBr2•glyme** (5 mol%) as pre-catalyst and **4CzIPN** (0.2 mol%) as photocatalyst. Both are air-stable solids used as received. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • The thiol coupling partner, **ethyl 3-mercaptopropionate**, is used in 1.5 equiv. to the aryl halide. Using less may favor disulfide formation. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • **Full procedure available in source file.**

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
• Demonstrated with an **aryl bromide** bearing an electron-withdrawing ester *para* to the halide. Electron-deficient arenes typically facilitate oxidative addition. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • Purity of the **aryl halide starting material** is critical. Impurities can lead to "slow reaction kinetics." Distillation of the electrophile is recommended if issues arise. (Note 5, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; 
### Functional Group Tolerance
• ✓ **Tolerates:** Ester (both on aryl halide and thiol partner). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • The reaction forms a thioether product; compatibility with other reducible or radical-sensitive functional groups (e.g., aldehydes, unhindered alkenes) under these photoredox conditions should be tested.

### Critical Conditions
• **Atmosphere:** Reaction must be degassed (via freeze-pump-thaw or repeated vacuum/nitrogen cycles) to exclude oxygen, which can quench the photoredox cycle. (Note 8, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • **Light Source:** Requires irradiation with a high-intensity **456 nm (blue) LED lamp** (40W Kessil PR160L) at close distance (~3 cm). Other wavelengths or lower intensities may fail. (Note 9, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • **Temperature:** Maintained at ~30°C with a nitrogen cooling stream. A "slight increase" in temperature is reported to increase kinetics without detriment. (Note 10, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • **Reaction Time:** Can be lengthy (24 hours for full conversion). Monitoring is essential as conversion is not linear; significant starting material may remain after 8 hours. (Note 11, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; 
### Side Reactions
• Major side reaction is **homocoupling of the thiol** to form the corresponding **disulfide** (diethyl 3,3'-disulfanediyldipropionate). Using excess thiol (1.5 equiv) helps suppress this. (Note 15 & 19, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • The disulfide byproduct must be separated during purification, as it can co-elute with the desired product on TLC/silica gel depending on the substrates. (Note 15 & 19, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142;

• Visual cues: Reaction mixture is initially a black suspension. A color change from brown to cyan is observed after several hours, indicating progression of the photoredox cycle. (Figure 2 & Note 11, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • **Full procedure available in source file.**

### Scale-up Notes
• Heat dissipation is crucial. At larger scales, maintaining ~30°C with a simple nitrogen stream may be insufficient; external cooling or more powerful lamps with integrated cooling may be needed. (Note 10, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • Photoreactor design (e.g., ensuring uniform light penetration and efficient stirring) will become a critical factor for reproducibility and yield on scale. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; 
### Analytical Notes
• **TLC:** Use 15% EtOAc/hexane. The desired product (Rf ~0.25) can be close to the disulfide byproduct (Rf ~0.38). **Stain with KMnO4** to clearly visualize the disulfide, which stains but may not be visible under UV. (Note 15, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • **GC/GC-MS** can be used to monitor substrate consumption and product formation. (Note 11, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • **Purification:** A slow gradient (2.5% to 20% EtOAc/hexane) is required for good separation of product from disulfide on silica gel. The disulfide elutes slightly earlier. (Note 18 & 19, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; 
### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
• **Low yield/conversion:** Check purity of aryl halide starting material. Impurities can severely slow kinetics; distill if necessary. (Note 5, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • **Disulfide formation dominant:** Ensure the reaction mixture is properly degassed and that a full 1.5 equivalents of thiol are used. Incomplete degassing can promote thiol oxidation. (Note 8 & 6, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142; • **Slow reaction:** Verify light source intensity and distance (~3 cm from vial). Ensure the reaction temperature is maintained; a slight increase to ~35-40°C may accelerate the reaction without harm. (Note 9 & 10, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 128-142;
