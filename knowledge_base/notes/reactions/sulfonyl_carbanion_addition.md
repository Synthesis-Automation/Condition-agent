### Reaction Type

A multistep synthesis of a 1-(phenylsulfonyl)bicyclo[1.1.0]butane scaffold from simple starting materials, involving deprotonation/sulfonyl carbanion addition to epichlorohydrin, sulfonate ester formation, and base-induced intramolecular nucleophilic substitution (cyclopropanation).
            `volume: 102`
  
### Mechanism Overview

* Step 1: Generation of a phenylsulfonyl carbanion (via deprotonation with di-n-butylmagnesium) adds regioselectively to the less hindered terminal carbon of epichlorohydrin, opening the epoxide and generating a chlorohydrin alkoxide. The alkoxide displaces chloride in an intramolecular S_N2 reaction to form a cyclobutanol. The steric bulk of the alkylmagnesium base is critical for clean deprotonation over nucleophilic attack on the epoxide. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202; *Step 2: The cyclobutanol is converted to a benzenesulfonate ester (tosylate analog) under mild conditions, activating the alcohol for subsequent elimination/substitution. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;*   Step 3: A strong base (n-BuLi) induces an intramolecular nucleophilic substitution, where the sulfonyl-stabilized carbanion displaces the sulfonate leaving group, forming the strained bicyclobutane core via an S_N2 pathway. This is the key C–C bond-forming cyclization step. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;

### Solvent Notes

* **THF (anhydrous, inhibitor-free)** — Essential for solubility of organometallic intermediates (Grignard reagent, carbanion) and for maintaining low temperatures effectively. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

* **Di-n-butylmagnesium** — Preferred over simpler Grignard reagents (like n-BuMgCl) for this deprotonation because its lower nucleophilicity and increased steric bulk favor deprotonation of the methyl sulfone over competing 1,2-addition to the epoxide. Its concentration and age are critical for reproducibility; batches should be titrated and used when >0.90 M. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202; ***n-Butyllithium** — Required for the final cyclization due to its strong basicity needed to generate the sulfonyl carbanion and drive the intramolecular displacement. Must be titrated before use for reliable stoichiometry. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;*   **Benzenesulfonyl chloride** — Acts as an activating agent for the secondary alcohol. Purity is important; purification through a basic alumina plug can improve final product purity and yield. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;

### Functional Group Tolerance

* **Tolerates:** Sulfone group (essential for carbanion stabilization), aryl sulfonate esters.
* **Likely Incompatible:** Protic functional groups (alcohols, amines, acids), electrophilic groups (aldehydes, ketones, unactivated esters), reducible groups (nitro, halides) that would react with the strongly basic and nucleophilic reagents.

### Critical Conditions

* **Atmosphere:** Strictly anhydrous conditions and an inert (N₂) atmosphere are mandatory for all steps involving organometallic reagents (n-Bu₂Mg, n-BuLi) to prevent quenching and side reactions. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202; ***Temperature Control:** Precise low-temperature control (-78 °C) is critical for the initial deprotonation/addition step to control selectivity. The final cyclization is initiated at -78 °C before warming to room temperature. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;*   **Reagent Concentration:** The active concentration of n-Bu₂Mg (must be >0.90 M) is a key variable for reproducible high yield. Old, diluted stocks lead to significant yield reduction. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;

### Side Reactions

* **Deprotonation vs. Addition:** If the alkylmagnesium base is not sufficiently bulky or is too nucleophilic, it may add directly to the epoxide instead of deprotonating the sulfone, leading to a different product manifold. Using fresh, concentrated n-Bu₂Mg mitigates this. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202; *   **Incomplete Activation/Sulfonation:** If the sulfonyl chloride is impure or wet, incomplete conversion of the cyclobutanol to the sulfonate ester will result, lowering the yield of the final cyclization. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;

### Procedure Hints

* **Key Observations:** After the initial n-Bu₂Mg addition at -78 °C, a white crystalline precipitate forms. After epichlorohydrin addition and warming, the mixture is a pale-green translucent solution with some solids; color hue can vary but is not an indicator of problems. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202; ***Addition Order:** The strong base (n-BuLi) for the final step must be added dropwise at -78 °C to control exotherm and ensure clean carbanion generation. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;*   **Workup:** Quenching with a "half-saturated" aqueous NH₄Cl solution effectively neutralizes excess base and lithium salts. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;

### Analytical Notes

* **TLC System:** Hexanes:EtOAc (2:1) is effective for monitoring all stages.
  * Methyl phenyl sulfone: R_f = 0.50
  * Intermediate A (Cyclobutanol): R_f = 0.10
  * Intermediate B (Sulfonate ester): R_f = 0.30
  * Product 1 (Bicyclobutane): R_f = 0.76
* If benzenesulfonyl chloride is visible on TLC, a less polar eluent (9:1 hexanes:EtOAc) can be used for clearer visualization. (Org. ### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips

* **Primary Yield Limitation:** The concentration/activity of the di-n-butylmagnesium reagent is the most critical factor. Always titrate and use a fresh, concentrated (>0.90 M) batch. Using the reagent in Et₂O/hexanes instead of heptane can increase yield by ~15%. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202; ***Purification Tip:** Dry-loading the crude product onto silica after pre-adsorption (concentrating with silica) provides a cleaner and more efficient chromatography separation. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;*   **Purity Improvement:** Pre-purifying benzenesulfonyl chloride by filtration through a basic alumina plug prior to use can lead to higher final product purity (>99.9%). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 185-202;
