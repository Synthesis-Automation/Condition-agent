### Reaction Type
Alkene dimesyloxylation (disulfonoxylation) using an iodine(III) reagent and a sulfonic acid.

### Mechanism Overview
*   The electrophilic iodine(III) reagent activates the alkene, followed by nucleophilic attack by the sulfonic acid to form a three-membered iodonium ion. A second sulfonic acid anion then opens this intermediate via backside attack, providing the 1,2-disulfonoxylation product with net anti addition. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; *   For less nucleophilic or "bulkier" sulfonic acids, a more electrophilic iodine(III) reagent like PhI(OAc)₂ (PIFA) is required to efficiently form the key iodonium intermediate. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; 
### Solvent Notes
*   Dichloromethane (DCM) — Suitable polar, aprotic solvent for the reaction; compatible with the iodine(III) oxidant and provides good solubility for alkene substrates and sulfonate products. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; 
### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
*   **Iodine(III) Reagent Selection**: Both iodomesitylene diacetate and (diacetoxyiodo)benzene (PIFA) are effective, commercially available oxidants. Iodomesitylene diacetate is generally effective, but PIFA is superior for less nucleophilic or sterically demanding sulfonic acids. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; *   **Sulfonic Acid Scope**: The reaction is general for various sulfonic acids (methanesulfonic, p-toluenesulfonic, 2-nitrobenzenesulfonic, etc.). The alkene is used as the limiting reagent, which is advantageous compared to prior methods requiring excess alkene. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; 
### Substrate Scope and Limitations
*   **Alkene Scope**: Terminal alkenes (e.g., 1-octene) work well. Internal and cyclic alkenes (e.g., cyclohexene) are also viable substrates, providing the corresponding 1,2-disulfonates. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; *   **Limitation**: The reaction is incompatible with alkenes containing strongly coordinating functional groups like free amines or carboxylic acids, which can interfere with the iodine(III) reagent. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; 
### Functional Group Tolerance
*   ✓ **Tolerates**: Aryl halides (F, Cl, Br), alkyl ethers, methoxy groups, alkyl chains. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; *   ✗ **Incompatible**: Free amines, free carboxylic acids. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; 
### Critical Conditions
*   **Order of Addition**: The sulfonic acid must be added *after* pre-mixing the alkene and iodine(III) reagent in the cooled solvent. Adding the acid first can lead to decomposition of the oxidant. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; *   **Temperature**: Initial cooling (0 °C) is recommended for reagent combination, but the reaction proceeds efficiently at room temperature (~22 °C) over 2 hours. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; 
### Side Reactions
*   No major side products are reported for the standard substrates under the optimized conditions. The main impurity after workup is residual aryl iodide (e.g., mesityl iodide) from the iodine(III) reagent, which is easily separated by chromatography. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; 
### Procedure Hints
*   A color change from colorless to yellow upon addition of methanesulfonic acid is a visual indicator of reagent mixing. This color fades to pale yellow as the reaction proceeds. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; *   **Workup Hint**: Quenching with saturated aqueous Na₂S₂O₃ and CH₂Cl₂ initially forms an emulsion where CH₂Cl₂ is the *top* layer. Diluting the aqueous phase with additional water reverses the phase densities, making CH₂Cl₂ the bottom layer and facilitating a clean separation. (Org. ### Scale-up Notes
*   The procedure is described on a 10 mmol scale. Concentration is relatively low (0.067 M in alkene) to manage potential exotherm and ensure efficient mixing. This concentration should be maintained or adjusted with care on larger scales. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; 
### Analytical Notes
*   **TLC**: The product (Rf ~0.3 in 1:2 EtOAc/hexanes) can be visualized with CAM or KMnO₄ stain. The starting alkene is not easily visualized by UV or common stains at reaction concentration. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; *   **NMR Monitoring**: Reaction progress is best monitored by ¹H NMR spectroscopy due to the poor TLC visualization of the starting alkene. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; *   The crude product is a mixture of the desired disulfonate and the aryl iodide byproduct. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; 
### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
*   Typical isolated yields are good to excellent (74% for the model substrate). A second run confirmed reproducibility. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; *   If low yields are observed with a bulky sulfonic acid, switching from iodomesitylene diacetate to the more electrophilic PIFA may improve results. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413; *   The crude product mixture may darken upon standing, but this does not affect the purity after purification. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 398-413;
