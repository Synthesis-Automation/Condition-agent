### Mechanism Overview

Key mechanistic steps, rate-limiting step, why these conditions matter electronically/sterically.

* The O-arylation of hydroxamic acids proceeds via deprotonation to form a nucleophilic amidate, which attacks an iodonium(III) electrophile; the strongly basic, non-nucleophilic potassium tert-butoxide is ideal for generating the reactive amidate anion. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155; *   The chlorination of N-alkoxy/aryloxy amides with PCl₅ proceeds through an imidoyl chloride intermediate; HCl is extruded as a gas, which is favored under the anhydrous, inert conditions. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155;

### Solvent Notes

Solvents to prefer or avoid, and the chemistry reason why.

* **Anhydrous ethanol** is suitable for O-arylation as it dissolves the potassium salt of benzohydroxamic acid. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155; *   For PCl₅ chlorinations, use **anhydrous chloroform stabilized with amylene**; avoid ethanol-stabilized or wet chloroform, as water hydrolyzes PCl₅ to POCl₃/HCl, and ethanol reacts to form POCl₃/HCl/EtCl, diminishing the chlorinating agent. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155;

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

Catalyst/ligand preferences, incompatibilities, or substrate-specific requirements.

* **Potassium tert-butoxide** should be freshly handled and used as received; it forms a cloudy suspension in ethanol that clarifies upon stirring, indicating salt formation/dissolution. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155; ***Diphenyliodonium chloride** can be added as a solid; its addition causes a color change (clear → yellow → pale brown) and the reaction mixture remains turbid, which is normal. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155;*   **Phosphorus pentachloride (PCl₅)** is highly moisture-sensitive and should be purged with N₂ stream prior to use to remove any HCl formed by atmospheric hydrolysis. ### Substrate Scope and Limitations

What substrate classes work, what are problematic, and what modifications help.

* The procedure is demonstrated for converting benzohydroxamic acid to the corresponding N-phenoxy amide and subsequently to the (Z)-imidoyl chloride. The method is general for preparing N-aryloxy imidoyl chlorides, which are key intermediates for generating N-alkoxy imidates and subsequent imidyl radicals. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155;

### Critical Conditions

Temperature, atmosphere, addition order, concentration, or timing effects.

* The **O-arylation step must be performed under an inert (N₂) atmosphere** to prevent side reactions of the basic reaction mixture. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155; *For the **PCl₅ chlorination, the amide must be added to the pre-cooled PCl₅/CHCl₃ solution at 0 °C**; HCl gas evolution begins immediately. The flask must be kept at 0 °C until gas evolution ceases to control the exotherm. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155;*   **Precipitation of the intermediate N-phenoxybenzamide during acidic workup requires slow addition of HCl** to afford a fine solid that minimizes trapped impurities. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155;

### Side Reactions

Known side reactions, what causes them, and how to suppress them.

* Hydrolysis of PCl₅ is a major side reaction if wet solvents or ethanol-stabilized chloroform are used, leading to diminished yield of the desired imidoyl chloride. Suppress by using rigorously dry, amylene-stabilized chloroform. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155; *   Decomposition or side reactions of the reactive imidoyl chloride product can occur if not handled under anhydrous conditions or if quenched improperly. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155;

### Procedure Hints

Addition order, atmosphere requirements, key observations (not full procedures).

* For O-arylation: Add diphenyliodonium chloride as a solid in portions over ~5 min under N₂ flow. The mixture will turn from clear to yellow to pale brown and remain turbid. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155; *For chlorination: After adding the amide to PCl₅ at 0 °C, wait for HCl gas evolution to stop before removing the ice bath and allowing the reaction to warm to room temperature. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155;*   Key observation: The final imidoyl chloride product is initially isolated as a colorless oil that solidifies upon storage at -20 °C. (Org. ### Scale-up Notes

What changes at larger scale: concentration, heat transfer, mixing, exotherm management.

* The exothermic HCl gas evolution during the PCl₅ chlorination step requires careful temperature control. On scale, efficient cooling and controlled addition of the solid amide will be critical to manage the gas release and heat. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155; *   The precipitation of the intermediate amide during workup involves adding acid to a basic aqueous solution. On scale, this should be done with good stirring and controlled addition to ensure formation of a filterable solid and avoid localized excess acid or base. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 143-155;
