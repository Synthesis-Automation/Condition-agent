### Reaction Type

Diisobutylaluminum hydride (DIBAL-H) reduction of esters to aldehydes.

### Mechanism Overview

* DIBAL-H delivers a hydride to the ester carbonyl, forming a tetrahedral alkoxyaluminum intermediate. A second equivalent can then over-reduce this intermediate to the alcohol, especially if the temperature is too high. The rate-limiting step is the initial hydride addition, and cryogenic conditions are critical to trap the aldehyde product after the first hydride transfer and prevent subsequent overreduction. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)

### Solvent Notes

* Dichloromethane (DCM) is a suitable solvent for DIBAL-H reductions at -78 °C, providing good solubility for both ester substrates and the reagent. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

* DIBAL-H is highly pyrophoric and moisture-sensitive. The reagent bottle must be secured with a clamp and its headspace kept under an inert atmosphere (argon/nitrogen) during use. (Note 5, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* Needles and syringes used to transfer DIBAL-H must be quenched immediately by flushing sequentially with hexanes and then a 1:9 isopropanol/hexanes solution to safely decompose residual reagent. (Note 5, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

* The method is demonstrated for the reduction of simple aliphatic esters (e.g., ethyl 3-phenylpropanoate) to aldehydes in high yield. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* The glass cannula transfer technique was developed to achieve selective mono-reduction in the presence of other sensitive electrophilic carbonyls in a complex molecule synthesis, indicating its utility for substrates prone to overreduction. (Discussion, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)

### Functional Group Tolerance

* The method is compatible with aromatic rings (phenyl).
* Overreduction to the alcohol is the primary incompatibility, which the procedure specifically aims to suppress via cryogenic addition. (Discussion, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)

### Critical Conditions

* Maintaining the internal reaction temperature at or below -75 °C during the addition of DIBAL-H is critical to suppress overreduction to the alcohol. (Note 7, Procedure, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* The reaction must be conducted under a strict inert atmosphere (argon/nitrogen) using flame-dried glassware to prevent quenching of the moisture-sensitive reagent. (Procedure, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* The use of a glass cannula for reagent transfer allows both the reagent solution and the reaction flask to be equilibrated in the same cooling bath, ensuring the reagent is added at the bath temperature and minimizing local warming. (Discussion, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)

### Side Reactions

* The primary side reaction is overreduction of the aldehyde product to the corresponding primary alcohol, caused by insufficiently low temperature during hydride addition. (Discussion, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* Quenching DIBAL-H reactions with aqueous acid can be vigorous. Using a saturated aqueous solution of sodium potassium tartrate (Rochelle's salt) provides a mild, slow quench that helps break down aluminum complexes and clarify the emulsion. (Procedure, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)

### Procedure Hints

* A glass cannula apparatus, where both flasks and the connecting tube are submerged in the cooling bath, prevents condensation of atmospheric moisture inside the transfer line, which could contaminate water-sensitive reactions. (Discussion, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* Transfer of the DIBAL-H solution via the cannula is initiated by temporarily closing the system to the argon manifold to allow a slight pressure build-up in the transfer flask, pushing the solution through. (Procedure, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* After the initial DIBAL-H addition, a second transfer with fresh DCM is used to quantitatively transfer any residual reagent. (Procedure, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)

### Analytical Notes

* TLC analysis (2:8 Et₂O/hexanes) can be misleading for monitoring DIBAL-H reductions because reduction can continue to occur on the silica spotter, making the reaction appear more complete than it is. Visualizing with both UV and KMnO₄ stain is recommended. (Note 8, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* For the model substrate, the ester starting material has Rf = 0.5 and the aldehyde product has Rf = 0.3 in 2:8 Et₂O/hexanes. (Note 8, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* Quantitative ¹H NMR using 1,3,5-trimethoxybenzene as an internal standard is an effective method for determining product purity. (Note 12, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips

* If the reaction temperature rises above -75 °C during addition (e.g., to -70 °C), overreduction is not necessarily observed, but tighter control is advised for optimal selectivity. (Note 7, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* Despite TLC potentially indicating completion after the reagent addition, stirring for an additional hour at -78 °C ensures full conversion of starting material. (Note 8, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* After workup and concentration, the product aldehyde is purified by filtration through a silica gel plug (not column chromatography) to avoid potential decomposition or loss of volatile material. (Procedure, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
* Trace solvent should be removed under high vacuum (e.g., 2 Torr), but prolonged exposure to high vacuum can lead to loss of the volatile aldehyde product. (Procedure, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 303)
