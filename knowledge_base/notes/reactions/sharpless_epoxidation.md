### Reaction Type

Sharpless Asymmetric Epoxidation; Sharpless Epoxidation of Allylic Alcohols.

### Mechanism Overview

* Catalytic cycle involves a titanium(IV)-dialkyl tartrate complex that coordinates the allylic alcohol substrate and delivers the peroxide oxygen enantioselectively. The tartrate ligand dictates the face of alkene approach.
* The rate-limiting step is the oxygen atom transfer from the coordinated alkyl hydroperoxide to the coordinated alkene. The reaction is sensitive to trace water, which hydrolyzes the active titanium catalyst and deactivates it. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;

### Solvent Notes

* **Dichloromethane (DCM)** — Standard solvent for Sharpless epoxidation; non-coordinating and dissolves both polar and non-polar components. Must be rigorously anhydrous. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; *   **Acetone** — Used in the workup to help solubilize and hydrolyze titanium aggregates into filterable solids. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

* **Titanium(IV) isopropoxide** — Must be freshly distilled to avoid hydrolysis products (e.g., titanium oxo clusters) which are less active or inactive. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; ***Dialkyl Tartrate (DIPT)** — The chiral ligand. High enantiomeric purity (>99% ee) is required to achieve high product ee. It is viscous; a large-gauge (e.g., 16-gauge) needle is recommended for accurate transfer. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;*   **Alkyl Hydroperoxide (TBHP or CHP)** — Acts as the stoichiometric oxidant. **Must be added to the organic material, never the reverse,** to minimize risk of uncontrolled exothermic decomposition. Can be used as a solution in decane (TBHP) or as a technical grade liquid (CHP, 80%). The choice affects downstream purification. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; *   **4Å Molecular Sieves (powdered)** — Critical for scavenging trace water from solvents and reagents to maintain catalyst activity. Must be activated (dried) prior to use. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

* **Works for:** Primary and secondary allylic alcohols. The procedure demonstrates high enantioselectivity (>99% ee) for divinyl carbinol (a primary, symmetrical bis-allylic alcohol). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; *   **Limitations:** The reaction is generally not effective for non-allylic alkenes or homoallylic alcohols. Epoxidation of trisubstituted alkenes in allylic alcohols can be slower and may give lower enantioselectivity. Full procedure available in source file.

### Functional Group Tolerance

* **✓ Tolerates:** Isolated alkenes (the less substituted one remains unreacted), hydroxyl group (the allylic alcohol is required).
* **✗ Incompatible:** Functional groups that coordinate strongly to titanium (e.g., free acids, amines, phosphines) will inhibit the reaction. Water leads to catalyst decomposition. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;

### Critical Conditions

* **Atmosphere:** Reactions must be set up under an inert atmosphere (nitrogen/argon) using Schlenk techniques or cannula transfer to exclude moisture and oxygen. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; ***Temperature:** Initial catalyst mixing is performed at -15 °C to -20 °C. The epoxidation itself proceeds over days at -20 °C; lower temperatures favor higher enantioselectivity. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;*   **Addition Order:** The standard order is: solvent/sieves → tartrate → Ti(OiPr)₄ → hydroperoxide → allylic alcohol. This allows the active titanium-tartrate complex to form before substrate addition. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; *   **Concentration:** A typical concentration is 0.5 M in the allylic alcohol. ### Side Reactions

* **Hydroperoxide Decomposition:** Alkyl hydroperoxides can decompose exothermically. Proper safety shielding is required, and they should always be added to the organic mixture. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; ***Formation of Alcohol Byproduct:** The reduced form of the hydroperoxide (e.g., *tert*-butanol from TBHP, 2-phenyl-2-propanol from CHP) is produced stoichiometrically and must be separated during purification, often by initial silica gel chromatography. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;*   **Low Enantioselectivity:** Caused by impure/racemic tartrate ligand, wet solvents/reagents, or improperly activated molecular sieves. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;

### Procedure Hints

* **Setup:** Use oven-dried glassware and cool under vacuum before refilling with inert gas. Seal joints/septa with electrical tape for long-term storage in a freezer. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; ***Monitoring:** Use a mini-workup (quenching an aliquot in NH₄Cl solution) for reliable TLC sampling to avoid spotting the active titanium complex directly. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;*   **Visual Cue:** The reaction mixture turns a deep orange upon addition of the allylic alcohol, indicating formation of the active catalyst-substrate complex. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; *   **Workup:** Adding acetone and aqueous citric acid hydrolyzes titanium aggregates into a filterable solid. Filtration through a celite pad is necessary to remove all fine particulate. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;

### Scale-up Notes

* **Exotherm Management:** The addition of the hydroperoxide is exothermic. For larger scales, slower addition with temperature control is critical to maintain the low temperature. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; *   **Mixing:** Efficient stirring is essential due to the heterogeneous mixture (molecular sieves). Ensure the stir bar can adequately suspend the solids. (Org. ### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips

* **Low Yield/Conversion:** Most common cause is the presence of water. Ensure all glassware is dry, solvents are anhydrous, and molecular sieves are freshly activated. Confirm Ti(OiPr)₄ is freshly distilled. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; ***Purification:** The alcohol byproduct (e.g., *tert*-butanol) must be separated by an initial silica gel column before distillation, as it can form an azeotrope with the product epoxide. The checkers found a step gradient (15% → 20% → 40% EtOAc/hexanes) effective when using cumene hydroperoxide. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;*   **Distillation Loss:** The product epoxide is volatile. During rotary evaporation, use minimal time, higher pressure (~100 mbar), and lower bath temperature (30-40 °C) to avoid significant co-evaporation into the cold trap. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18; *   **Distillation Technique:** Apply vacuum slowly at room temperature to prevent bumping. Use a short-path distillation apparatus with an ice-water cooled condenser and collect fractions carefully. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 1-18;
