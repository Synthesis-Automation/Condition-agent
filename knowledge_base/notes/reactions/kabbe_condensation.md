### Reaction Type
Kabbe condensation (modified using pyrrolidine and butyric acid in DMSO).  

### Mechanism Overview
*   Key step involves pyrrolidine enamine formation from the methyl ketone (6-methyl-5-hepten-2-one), followed by conjugate addition to the ortho-hydroxyacetophenone activated by intramolecular hydrogen bonding. The butyric acid acts as a co-catalyst, likely by forming an ammonium salt with pyrrolidine, which may help control enamine formation and moderate basicity. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)
*   The use of pyrrolidine over simpler secondary amines may offer better nucleophilicity and a defined enamine geometry, critical for the desired cyclization. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)

### Solvent Notes
*   **DMSO is the optimal solvent**; it gave better results than other high-boiling polar aprotic solvents tested (DMI, DMA, sulfolane, acetonitrile). The high polarity likely facilitates the ionic intermediates and transition states. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)
*   **DMI (N,N′-dimethylimidazolidinone) can be a suitable alternative** but must be used from a fresh, unopened bottle, as an old opened bottle led to decreased yields, suggesting sensitivity to moisture or degradation over time. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
*   **Pyrrolidine purity is critical.** Using unpurified pyrrolidine gave inconsistent yields. It must be freshly distilled (e.g., via Hickman distillation or short-path distillation) to ensure successful and reproducible transformation. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)
*   **Butyric acid is used as a 0.5 equiv additive.** Its role is likely to form an ammonium carboxylate salt with pyrrolidine, which modulates reactivity and may prevent over-enolization or side reactions. Significant gas evolution (presumably CO₂) is observed upon mixing, which is a useful visual indicator of the acid-base reaction. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)

*   **Tolerates:** Alkene, aryl methyl ether, phenol (as a reactive site). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)

### Critical Conditions
*   **Reaction atmosphere:** The reaction is performed under an inert atmosphere (nitrogen), which is necessary due to the use of sensitive reagents like the pyrrolidine enamine. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)
*   **Addition order is critical.** The acid/base mixture (pyrrolidine/butyric acid) is pre-formed and allowed to stir before adding the methyl ketone to form the enamine, which then reacts with the acetophenone. Adding components out of sequence can compromise the yield. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)
*   **Reaction progress is indicated by distinct color changes:** from colorless to pale yellow upon enamine formation, then to orange/red-orange immediately upon acetophenone addition, providing a useful visual monitor. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)

### Side Reactions
*   If the reaction does not go to completion, the major impurity is unreacted phenolic starting material, which can be removed during workup with a base wash. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)

### Procedure Hints
*   For the aqueous workup, a large dilution (100 mL water per mmol) is used to ensure product extraction into ethyl acetate and to dissolve DMSO. The organic layer takes time to separate cleanly. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)
*   During column chromatography, product bands may be colorless or faintly yellow, while unreacted starting material elutes as a bright yellow band. An impurity just below the product on TLC may only be visible with a permanganate stain, not UV, requiring careful fraction analysis. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)

### Analytical Notes
*   TLC (9:1 Hexanes:EtOAc): Product Rf = 0.43; Starting Material Rf = 0.31. The product is strongly visible under long-wave (365 nm) UV light, while the starting material is not. Both are visible under short-wave (254 nm) UV light. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)
*   Unreacted phenolic starting material can be detected in the crude mixture by ¹H NMR via a characteristic phenol peak near 12 ppm. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)
*   Purity can be accurately determined by quantitative ¹H NMR using dimethyl terephthalate as an internal standard (comparing integrals at 3.79 ppm and 3.94 ppm). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
*   The primary yield limitation is incomplete reaction. If residual starting material is present (indicated by NMR), it can be efficiently removed by washing the crude mixture dissolved in ethyl acetate with 15 wt% aqueous NaOH (3-5 times). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)
*   Consistent high yields depend on using freshly distilled pyrrolidine and fresh, anhydrous DMSO (or a fresh bottle of DMI). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 335-349)
