### Reaction Type

Reduction of carboxylic acids to primary alcohols using in situ generated borane-ammonia as the stoichiometric reductant and TiCl₄ as a Lewis acid catalyst.

### Mechanism Overview

• TiCl₄ activates the carboxylic acid by coordinating to the carbonyl oxygen, enhancing its electrophilicity for hydride attack from borane-ammonia. The rate-limiting step likely involves the transfer of hydride to the activated carbonyl complex. The TiCl₄ catalyst is essential for the reduction of relatively unreactive carboxylic acids, as borane-ammonia alone is insufficient. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19;

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

• **Borane-Ammonia (BH₃·NH₃)**: Functions as a stable, solid source of borane (BH₃). It must be finely powdered for efficient reaction; coarse material leads to longer reaction times and low yields. It contains minor BHT if stabilized THF is used in its preparation. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 3, 21)
• **TiCl₄**: Serves as a Lewis acid catalyst (0.15 equiv is sufficient). It must be handled with care and added dropwise to a cooled solution to control exotherm and side reactions. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Procedure B)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

• Works well for carboxylic acids, including N-protected amino acids (e.g., Boc-glycine). The method is demonstrated for converting an N-Boc-protected amino acid to the corresponding N-Boc-amino alcohol without epimerization. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Title, Procedure B)
• The specific scope beyond Boc-protected glycine is not detailed in the provided text, but the title indicates general applicability to carboxylic acids. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Title)

### Functional Group Tolerance

✓ **Tolerates**: tert-Butoxycarbonyl (Boc) protecting group on nitrogen. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Procedure B)
✗ **Incompatible**: The reductant (borane-ammonia) is incompatible with protic solvents (water, alcohols) and acids/oxidants, which will decompose it with hydrogen gas evolution. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 16)

### Critical Conditions

• **Atmosphere**: The borane-ammonia synthesis and the reduction reaction must be conducted in a well-ventilated fume hood due to significant hydrogen gas evolution. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 4, 19)
• **Temperature**: The borane-ammonia synthesis is exothermic upon water addition and must be performed at 0 °C. The subsequent reduction is initiated at 0 °C before warming to room temperature. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Procedure A, B)
• **Order of Addition**: For the reduction, TiCl₄ must be added to the cooled acid solution first to form the activated complex, followed by the slow, portionwise addition of solid borane-ammonia. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Procedure B)

### Side Reactions

• Vigorous **hydrogen gas evolution** occurs during the synthesis of borane-ammonia (from NaBH₄ + (NH₄)₂SO₄ + H₂O), during its addition to the reaction mixture, and upon acidic quench. This can cause frothing and requires controlled addition. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 6, 20, 23)

### Procedure Hints

• For borane-ammonia synthesis, **very vigorous stirring** (1000 rpm) of the heterogeneous mixture is essential to complete the reaction in 4-6 hours. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 9)
• Add borane-ammonia reductant to the reaction in **small portions** to prevent excessive foaming and over-reaction. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 20)
• **Quenching** with cold aqueous HCl must be done slowly at 0 °C to manage hydrogen gas evolution. The resulting color (blue to white) is variable and does not affect yield. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 23, 24)

### Analytical Notes

• **Borane-ammonia synthesis progress** is monitored by ¹¹B NMR, specifically by the disappearance of the NaBH₄ peak (~ -43.6 ppm) and appearance of the BH₃·NH₃ quartet (~ -22 ppm). Adding a drop of DMSO to an aliquot dissolves NaBH₄ for accurate analysis. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 8)
• **Reduction progress** is monitored by ¹H NMR for the disappearance of the starting carboxylic acid signal. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 22)
• Purity of borane-ammonia can be determined quantitatively by **hydride analysis**. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 14)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips

• Low yield in borane-ammonia synthesis can result from using **non-powdered ammonium sulfate**; it must be finely ground to a powder to achieve good yield and reasonable reaction time. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 3)
• If the reduction mixture becomes an **extremely viscous slurry** upon borane-ammonia addition, the reductant was likely not finely powdered enough. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 21)
• Diethyl ether can be substituted with **methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE)** as the solvent for the reduction with a moderate drop in yield (94% vs 74%). (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 19; Note 30)
