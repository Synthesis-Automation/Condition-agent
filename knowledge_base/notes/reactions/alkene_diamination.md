### Reaction Type
Stereospecific anti-diamination of alkenes using iodine catalysis and a nitrosamide nitrogen source.

### Mechanism Overview
*   Iodine catalyzes the formation of a chloramine-type intermediate from nosylamide and NaOCl·5H₂O, which then adds across the alkene in a stereospecific *anti* fashion, likely via an iodonium ion intermediate that is opened by the second nitrogen nucleophile. The stereochemistry of the alkene is transferred to the diamine product. (Minakata, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 203-216; 
### Solvent Notes
*   Acetonitrile is used as the solvent, likely due to its ability to dissolve both the organic reactants and the ionic iodine catalyst/oxidant system while promoting the ionic halogenation/diamination pathway. (Minakata, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 203-216; 
### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
*   **Iodine Catalyst:** Serves as a Lewis acid to activate the halogen source (NaOCl). A catalytic amount (10 mol%) is sufficient. (Minakata, Org. It must be weighed and handled quickly with non-metallic tools (plastic/glass) due to its hygroscopicity and corrosivity. Contact with metals or fabrics must be avoided. (Minakata, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 203-216; *   **Nosylamide (2-nitrobenzenesulfonamide):** Acts as the bidentate nitrogen source. The electron-withdrawing nosyl (Ns) groups activate the N-H bonds for oxidation and facilitate subsequent purification and eventual deprotection of the diamine product. (Minakata, Org. ### Substrate Scope and Limitations
*   **Scope:** The method is demonstrated for *trans*-β-methylstyrene. The discussion indicates it works for both E and Z alkenes with complete stereospecificity (giving *anti* addition products) and is applicable to cyclic alkenes. It provides access to 1,2-diamines from readily available alkene starting materials. (Minakata, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 203-216; *   **Limitations:** The procedure uses a stabilized alkene (styrene derivative). Performance with unactivated, aliphatic alkenes is not detailed in this specific procedure but is referenced from prior work. The presence of alkene stabilizers (e.g., 4-tert-butylcatechol) does not interfere. (Minakata, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 203-216; 
### Functional Group Tolerance

This order likely ensures generation of the active chloramine species before introduction of the alkene. (Minakata, Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 203-216;
