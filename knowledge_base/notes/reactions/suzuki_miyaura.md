### Reaction Type

Copper-catalyzed Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling of unactivated alkyl halides with arylboronic acid esters.

### Mechanism Overview

* The copper(I)/bathophenanthroline catalyst system facilitates oxidative addition into unactivated alkyl halides (including chlorides) and transmetalation with arylboron reagents. The use of a strong alkoxide base (NaOtBu) is critical for activating the boron reagent and generating the active aryl nucleophile. The key advantage is avoiding the β-hydride elimination pathway common with other metals, enabling the use of secondary alkyl halides. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99;

### Solvent Notes

* **Toluene** is the preferred solvent for this copper-catalyzed alkyl–aryl coupling. It effectively solubilizes the organic substrates and ligand while being compatible with the strong alkoxide base. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99; *   The procedure specifies drying toluene with anhydrous MgSO₄ overnight prior to use, indicating that anhydrous conditions are beneficial, though the reaction itself is run under air. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99;

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

* **Catalyst:** Copper(I) bromide–dimethyl sulfide complex (CuBr·SMe₂) is used as the copper source. The dimethyl sulfide ligand likely aids in stabilizing the Cu(I) species. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99; ***Ligand:** Bathophenanthroline (4,7-diphenyl-1,10-phenanthroline) is essential. Its bidentate chelating structure and extended aromatic system are optimal for stabilizing the copper catalyst and promoting the cross-coupling over side reactions. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99;*   **Base:** Sodium *tert*-butoxide (NaOtBu) is required in significant excess (1.5 equiv relative to alkyl halide). It serves to activate the boron reagent via transmetalation. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99; *   **Boron Reagent:** Arylboronic acid pinacol esters are used. An excess (1.8 equiv) is explicitly recommended to compensate for a side homocoupling reaction of the boron reagent. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99; Note 3)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

* **Works:** The method is general for **unactivated primary and secondary alkyl halides** (bromides, chlorides, iodides). Cyclic and acyclic alkyl bromides are well-tolerated. The system shows broad functional group tolerance. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99; *   The discussion indicates the method is significant because it addresses the historical challenge of using alkyl halides in Suzuki-Miyaura couplings, which often suffer from slow oxidative addition and competing β-hydride elimination. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99;

### Critical Conditions

* **Atmosphere:** A key feature is that the reaction **does not require an inert atmosphere** (e.g., N₂ or Ar) and can be set up under air, greatly simplifying operations. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99; ***Temperature/Time:** The reaction requires heating at **80 °C for 24 hours**, indicating it is not instantaneous and benefits from prolonged heating. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99;*   **Vessel:** A **heavy-walled pressure vessel** is used, likely as a safety precaution for heating volatile solvents like toluene in a sealed system, not because high pressure is mechanistically required. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99;

### Side Reactions

* **Homocoupling of the arylboron reagent** is a known side reaction. This is why an excess (1.8 equiv) of the boron reagent is used to drive the cross-coupling to completion. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99; Note 3)

### Procedure Hints

* The reaction mixture turns **black** upon heating and remains black upon completion, which is a normal observation. (Org. ### Analytical Notes

* **TLC:** The product (1-cyclohexyl-4-chlorobenzene) has an Rf = **0.8** using distilled petroleum ether as the eluent. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 86-99; Note 12)

The first column gave the bulk of the product (65%), and a second column on the mixed fractions yielded an additional 8%. (Org.
