Common Reagents and Conditions from the Roche DUCK Study

A recommendation-oriented summary of the detailed reagent statistics reported for N-acylation to amide and Suzuki coupling.

Scope limitation: the paper and SI report detailed reagent/base statistics for these two reaction families, but do not publish complete distributions of solvent, temperature, reaction time, concentration, equivalents, or atmosphere. The information below is best used as an enterprise-prevalence prior, not as a complete optimized protocol.

3. How to Use These Data in a Recommendation System

Recommended scoring model

Condition score = w₁ × precedent frequency + w₂ × substrate similarity + w₃ × observed outcome + w₄ × recency

Suggested recommendation tiers

TierPurposeExamples
1Most common general priorHATU/DIPEA; PdCl₂(dppf)/K₂CO₃
2Common mechanistically distinct alternativeT3P/DIPEA; Pd(OAc)₂/Na₂CO₃
3Modern or challenging-substrate optionTCFH systems; XPhos Pd G3/Cs₂CO₃
4Specialized activation pathwayAcid chloride route; K₃PO₄-based Suzuki

Important data cautions

  • Popularity is not equivalent to optimality. Common conditions may reflect historical habit and operational convenience.
  • Missing yield must not be treated as failure. The paper assigned unannotated yields to zero for some analyses.
  • Catalyst and ligand should be separate fields. Additional ligands may not always be captured by catalyst CAS-based grouping.
  • Outcome comparisons are observational. Different condition sets were likely used on different substrate classes.
  • Use recency weighting. Emerging systems such as XPhos Pd G3 and 1-methylimidazole may be underrepresented in cumulative counts.

Recommended outcome labels

outcome_status = measured_success measured_partial measured_failure yield_missing

4. Other High-Priority Reaction Families

These reaction classes are frequent or increasing in the DUCK dataset, but the paper and SI do not provide enough detailed condition distributions to build complete recommendation rules.

  • Carboxylic acid + amine condensation
  • N-Boc deprotection
  • Chloro N-arylation
  • Bromo Suzuki-type coupling
  • Reductive amination
  • N-sulfonylation

These families are useful for prioritizing future dataset collection, but their solvent, temperature, catalyst loading, time, and concentration rules require other reaction-level datasets.

Source: “Charting the evolving chemical synthesis repertoire at Roche” and its Supporting Information. This page summarizes source-reported aggregate statistics for recommendation-system design.
