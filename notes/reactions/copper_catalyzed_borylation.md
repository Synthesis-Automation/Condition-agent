### Reaction Type
This document describes a scaleable synthesis of alkylboronates via Cu-catalyzed borylation of primary alkyl halides, often termed copper-catalyzed alkylboration or hydroboration. It represents a practical variant of metal-catalyzed sp³–sp² borylation reactions.

### Mechanism Overview
The mechanism proceeds via single-electron transfer (SET) from a Cu(I) species to the alkyl halide, generating an alkyl radical and Cu(II). This radical adds to the diboron reagent (B₂pin₂), and the resulting boryl radical is quenched by hydrogen atom transfer (HAT) from the solvent or a sacrificial hydrogen donor (e.g., pinacolborane, HBpin), regenerating the Cu(I) catalyst. The use of a strong base (NaOtBu) is critical for generating the active Cu(I) catalyst and potentially for activating the diboron reagent. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Solvent Notes
✓ Good: **Diglyme** — High boiling point (162°C) is suitable for the required reaction temperature (130°C) and may help stabilize copper complexes. It serves as an effective solvent for this Cu-catalyzed borylation. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ Avoid: **Ethereal solvents (Et₂O, THF)** — Lower boiling points preclude reaching the necessary reaction temperature (130°C). (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
✓ **Catalyst:** CuI is effective and economical. It must be used with a suitable ligand (see below) to form the active complex. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ **Ligand:** **4,4'-Di-tert-butyl-2,2'-bipyridine (dtbpy)** is optimal. Its steric bulk and electron-donating properties stabilize the copper catalyst and enhance activity, suppressing side reactions like homocoupling of alkyl halides. Other bipyridine ligands (e.g., bpy, dmbpy) give inferior results. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ **Boron Source:** **Bis(pinacolato)diboron (B₂pin₂)** is the standard reagent. The presence of **pinacolborane (HBpin)** as an impurity or additive acts as a critical hydrogen atom donor to terminate the radical chain, preventing side reactions and improving yield. Commercial B₂pin₂ often contains sufficient HBpin. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ **Base:** **Sodium tert-butoxide (NaOtBu)** is required. It likely generates the active Cu(I)-alkoxide species and may activate B₂pin₂. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
✓ Works well for **primary alkyl iodides and bromides**. Alkyl iodides generally react faster. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ **Primary alkyl chlorides and secondary/tertiary alkyl halides** are not viable substrates under these conditions. Secondary halides are prone to elimination and slower radical generation. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ **Benzylic and alkoxy-substituted primary alkyl halides** are competent substrates, demonstrating good functional group tolerance. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Functional Group Tolerance
✓ Tolerates: **ether, ester, acetal, silyl ether, alkyl chloride (remote), epoxide**. The method is notably tolerant of heteroatoms and polar functional groups. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ Incompatible: **Strongly coordinating groups (e.g., free acids, amines)** were not tested but could potentially inhibit the copper catalyst. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Critical Conditions
* **Temperature:** **130°C** is essential for efficient reaction. Lower temperatures (e.g., 110°C) result in significantly lower conversion. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **Atmosphere:** Reactions are performed under an inert atmosphere (**N₂ or Ar**) to prevent catalyst oxidation and decomposition of oxygen-sensitive reagents. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **Reaction Vessel:** A **sealed pressure vessel** (e.g., a heavy-walled vial) is required to contain the volatile solvent and reagents at 130°C. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Side Reactions
* **Homocoupling of the alkyl halide** (Wurtz-type coupling) is a major side reaction. It is suppressed by using the **dtbpy ligand**, which favors the desired borylation pathway over halogen atom transfer. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **Alkene formation via β-hydride elimination** from the alkyl radical or alkyl-copper intermediate is possible, especially for substrates that can form stable alkenes. The dtbpy ligand and the presence of a H-atom donor (HBpin) help minimize this. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Procedure Hints
* The order of addition is important: add the **NaOtBu last**, just before sealing the vessel, as it is highly reactive/moisture-sensitive. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* The reaction mixture typically turns **dark brown/black upon heating**, which is normal. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* Maintain a strict **inert atmosphere** throughout setup. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Scale-up Notes
* The procedure is designed for scale. At the 20 mmol scale, a **100 mL Parr pressure vessel** is used to safely contain the pressure. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **Magnetic stirring may be insufficient** at larger scales; mechanical stirring is recommended for adequate mixing. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **Heat-up and cool-down times** will be longer; ensure the reaction is held at 130°C for the full duration. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Analytical Notes
* **TLC** (hexanes/EtOAc) can monitor consumption of alkyl halide (UV-active) and formation of the alkylboronate (visualized with **Pd stain**, e.g., PdCl₂ in acidic MeOH, which gives a brown spot). The boronate typically has a higher Rf than the starting halide. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **¹¹B NMR** is a direct method for characterizing the product, with alkyl-Bpin species resonating around δ 33 ppm. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **¹H NMR** after aqueous workup shows characteristic peaks for the pinacolato group (~1.2-1.3 ppm). (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
* **Ensure B₂pin₂ contains HBpin.** If yields are low, adding **1-5 mol% of HBpin** can significantly improve the reaction by ensuring an efficient HAT terminus. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **Use fresh, high-quality dtbpy ligand.** Ligand quality is crucial for achieving high yields and suppressing homocoupling. (Org. Synth. 2025)
* **Incomplete conversion:** If starting material remains, verify the reaction temperature is truly 130°C and consider a longer reaction time (up to 48h). (Org. Synth. 2025)
* For acid-sensitive substrates, a **milder aqueous workup** (using sat. aq. NaHCO₃ instead of HCl) is recommended to prevent protodeboronation. (Org. Synth. 2025)
