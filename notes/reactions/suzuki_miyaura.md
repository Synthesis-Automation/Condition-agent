### Reaction Type
Copper-catalyzed Suzuki–Miyaura cross-coupling of unactivated alkyl halides with arylboronates

### Solvent Notes
• Use anhydrous, nonpolar solvents (toluene dried over MgSO₄) to minimize boronate hydrolysis and base quenching (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Avoid protic or highly coordinating solvents (e.g. DMF, MeOH) that can deactivate Cu–ligand complexes or promote protodeboronation (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
• Cu(I) salts with diimine ligands (CuBr·SMe₂ + bathophenanthroline) enable challenging sp³ C–X oxidative addition and suppress β-hydride elimination (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Strong, hindered alkoxide bases (NaOtBu) are essential for efficient transmetalation; weaker bases give low conversion (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Employ ≥1.5 equiv of base and ≥1.8 equiv of boronic ester to compensate for competitive homocoupling (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Side Reactions
• Homocoupling of arylboronates increases with insufficient base or ligand; suppress by excess boron reagent and optimized Cu/ligand ratio (Org. Synth. 2025)
• β-Hydride elimination from alkyl–Cu intermediates can erode yields; minimized by proper ligand choice and controlled temperature (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
• Unactivated primary and secondary alkyl bromides can be coupled, but secondary substrates are more prone to β-hydride elimination (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Arylboronic pinacol esters tolerate electron-donating and -withdrawing substituents; pinacol protection improves stability vs. free boronic acids (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Critical Conditions
• Conduct reaction in a sealed, oven-dried pressure vessel to maintain anhydrous conditions and allow heating to 80 °C for 24 h (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Charge base, catalyst, ligand, and boronate under air but ensure solvent and glassware are rigorously dried (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Monitor reaction color (black slurry indicates active catalyst species) and use TLC (Rf ~0.8 in PE) to track consumption (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
• Typical isolated yields are 65–73%; small‐scale runs can give up to 78% yield (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Glassware and stir bars must be oven‐dried to prevent moisture; incomplete drying leads to lower conversion (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Filtration through silica/diatomite removes copper salts and base; multiple chromatography steps may be needed to separate homocoupling byproducts (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Reaction Type
Palladium/biarylphosphine-catalyzed Suzuki coupling of aryl mesylates with arylboronic acids

### Solvent Notes
• Good: CH2Cl2 — anhydrous dichloromethane dissolves Pd(OAc)2 and ligand for rapid precatalyst formation (Org. Synth.)
• Good: tBuOH — protic high-bp solvent dissolves inorganic bases (K2CO3, K3PO4) in Suzuki couplings; use blast shield above 82 °C due to pressure buildup (Org. Synth.)
• Et3N — dual role as base and co-solvent for catalyst activation; ensure dryness to prevent catalyst deactivation (Org. Synth.)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
• Pre-activate Pd(OAc)2 (1 mol%) with biaryl phosphine (CM-Phos, 4 mol%) in CH2Cl2/Et3N at 50 °C for 5 min, then remove volatiles to isolate active precatalyst (Org. Synth.)
• For aryl mesylates, employ 1 mol% Pd and 4 mol% ligand to overcome the low reactivity of sulfonate leaving groups (Org. Synth.)
• Use 2–3 equiv of arylboronic acid in Suzuki couplings of aryl mesylates to drive conversion and suppress homocoupling (Org. Synth.)
• K3PO4 for Suzuki couplings—readily available and effective with tBuOH (Org. Synth.)
• Dry and degas all solvents and reagents (e.g., distill tBuOH from Na, Et3N from KOH, CH2Cl2 from CaH2) to avoid catalyst poisoning (Org. Synth.)

### Side Reactions
• Poor stirring on scale leads to sticky, glutinous mixtures and lower yields—ensure vigorous agitation (Org. Synth.)
• Sealed-vessel heating of tBuOH above its bp can cause dangerous overpressure—use blast shield and rated glassware (Org. Synth.)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
• Aryl mesylates bearing electron-neutral or weakly donating groups (e.g., t-Bu) couple efficiently under these Pd/CM-Phos conditions (Org. Synth.)
• Large-scale reactions require longer times (24 h) than small-scale examples to reach full conversion (Org. Synth.)
• Sterically hindered or deactivated aryl sulfonates may need increased ligand loading or temperature (Org. Synth.)

### Critical Conditions
• Rigorously exclude air and moisture: evacuate/backfill flask three times with N2 prior to catalyst activation and substrate addition (Org. Synth.)
• After precatalyst formation, strip volatiles at high vacuum (0.01 mmHg) to obtain a solid pre-catalyst, then add substrate/base/solvent (Org. Synth.)
• Pre-stir at room temperature for 10 min before ramping to 120 °C for 24 h; this "settling" period improves reproducibility (Org. Synth.)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
• Rapid TLC monitoring of mesylate formation (Rf differentiation in EtOAc–DCM–hexanes) avoids over-reaction or degradation (Org. Synth.)
• Pre-distillation or purification of phenylboronic acid enhances yield by minimizing proto-deboronation (Org. Synth.)
• Dry crude product under high vacuum after evaporation to remove residual tBuOH or Et3N, improving downstream yields (Org. Synth.)

### Reaction Type
Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling catalyzed by nickel; compatible with heterocyclic halides and phenol-derived electrophiles

### Solvent Notes
• t-Amyl alcohol (2-methylbutan-2-ol) is an effective "green" solvent for nickel-catalyzed couplings; compatible with heterocyclic substrates (Source document)
• 2-Me-THF is another viable "green" solvent offering an alternative to traditional ethereal solvents (Source document)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
• NiCl₂(PCy₃)₂ is a highly effective pre-catalyst for heterocyclic halides and phenolic derivatives (sulfamates, carbamates) at 0.5–1 mol% loadings (Source document)
• Anhydrous K₃PO₄ is a suitable base; anhydrous material eliminates bubbling observed during flame-drying steps (Source document)
• Heterocyclic boronic acids (e.g., 3-furanylboronic acid) are competent coupling partners under these conditions (Source document)

### Side Reactions
• High yields and clean profiles with NiCl₂(PCy₃)₂ / t-amyl alcohol / K₃PO₄ effectively suppress protodeboronation and homocoupling for these substrate classes (Source document)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
• Broadly applicable to heterocyclic halides (Cl, Br, I) and phenol-derived electrophiles (carbamates, sulfamates, mesylates, pivalates) (Source document)
• Particularly useful for bis(heterocyclic) frameworks that are challenging via traditional palladium catalysis (Source document)

### Critical Conditions
• Inert atmosphere (N₂ or Ar): evacuate/backfill cycles on solid mixture before solvent addition (Procedure)
• Pre-stir at room temperature for 1 h after mixing all components before heating (Procedure)
• Heat at 100–120 °C for completion; use excess boronic acid (2.5–4.0 equiv) and base (4.5–7.2 equiv) for challenging substrates (Procedure)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
• Wash organic extract with 1.0 M NaOH to remove boronic acid residues and acidic byproducts (Procedure)
• Procedure is scalable; consistent yields observed at half-scale (Note 8)

### Reaction Type
Suzuki–Miyaura cross-coupling reaction.

### Mechanism Overview
Oxidative addition of the secondary alkyl electrophile (bromide) to Pd(0) is likely rate-limiting. The use of an electron-rich, bulky biaryl phosphine ligand (SPhos) facilitates this step by promoting reductive elimination and stabilizing the Pd(0) state. The phosphate ester leaving group on the boronic ester coupling partner enhances transmetalation efficiency by acting as a directing group and stabilizing the tetracoordinate boronate intermediate. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)

### Solvent Notes
✓ Good: **1,4-Dioxane** — Provides optimal solubility for all components (polar substrates, inorganic base) and maintains a single phase under heating, crucial for efficient coupling. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
✗ Avoid: **THF, Et₂O, toluene** — Tested but provided lower yields, likely due to poorer solubility of the inorganic base (Cs₂CO₃) and substrates. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
✓ **Catalyst:** Pd(OAc)₂/SPhos is the optimal system. The SPhos ligand is critical for suppressing β-hydride elimination from the secondary alkylpalladium intermediate. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
✓ **Base:** Cs₂CO₃ is optimal. K₃PO₄ and K₂CO₃ gave lower yields. The choice of carbonate base is crucial, affecting both the transmetalation rate and the stability of the boronic ester. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
✗ **Boronic ester stability:** The neopentyl glycol-derived arylboronic ester is prone to protodeboronation under basic aqueous workup conditions. Immediate extraction after reaction quenching is necessary to preserve yield. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
✓ **Works well:** Coupling of secondary alkyl bromides (including those α to oxygen) with aryl boronic esters bearing a phosphate ester directing group. The phosphate ester is a key design element for success. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
✗ **Problematic:** The methodology is specifically designed for substrates containing the phosphate ester moiety. Unfunctionalized aryl boronic acids/esters likely do not perform as well under these conditions. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)

### Functional Group Tolerance
✓ **Tolerates:** Phosphate ester (acts as directing group), ether, secondary alkyl bromide. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
⚠ **Conditionally compatible:** Boronic ester (neopentyl glycol ester) — stable under reaction conditions but base-sensitive during workup. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
✗ **Not tested/incompatible:** The presence of the phosphate ester is essential; tolerance of other strong coordinating groups (e.g., free acids, amines) or reducible groups (e.g., aldehydes, nitro) is not demonstrated. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)

### Critical Conditions
✓ **Temperature:** 100 °C is optimal. Lower temperatures (80 °C) result in significantly lower conversion. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
✓ **Atmosphere:** The reaction is performed under an inert atmosphere (N₂ or Ar) in a sealed pressure vessel to prevent catalyst oxidation/deactivation and solvent loss. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
✓ **Concentration:** 0.1 M was used successfully. Concentration is important for ensuring sufficient interaction between poorly soluble Cs₂CO₃ and the reaction mixture. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)

### Side Reactions
✓ **Protodeboronation:** The primary side reaction is base-mediated protodeboronation of the boronic ester, primarily occurring during aqueous workup, not during the reaction itself. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
✓ **β-Hydride Elimination:** Suppressed effectively by the use of the SPhos ligand. Using less effective ligands (e.g., XPhos) leads to elimination products. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)

### Procedure Hints
• The reaction mixture is heterogeneous due to the solid Cs₂CO₃. Vigorous stirring is essential. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
• Use a pressure vessel (sealed tube) to safely heat dioxane to 100 °C and maintain a single-phase system. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
• Quench with water and extract *immediately* into organic solvent to minimize boronic ester decomposition. Do not let the quenched mixture stand. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)

### Scale-up Notes
• **Heat Transfer/Mixing:** On scale, ensuring efficient heat transfer into the heterogeneous mixture and maintaining vigorous agitation for solid-liquid contact are key considerations. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
• **Pressure:** The use of a pressure-rated reactor (e.g., Parr vessel) is necessary for safe scale-up due to the solvent's boiling point and sealed conditions. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
• **Exotherm:** Not reported, but the reaction is heated, not exothermic. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)

### Analytical Notes
• Reaction progress can be monitored by LCMS, observing consumption of the alkyl bromide starting material (M+Br isotopic pattern) and appearance of the product mass. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
• TLC (Hexanes/EtOAc) was used but not explicitly described; LCMS was the primary analytical tool. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
✓ **Key to High Yield:** The combination of SPhos ligand, Cs₂CO₃ base, dioxane solvent, and the phosphate ester directing group is synergistic. Omitting any component reduces yield. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
✓ **Workup is Critical:** The greatest potential for yield loss is during workup due to boronic ester hydrolysis. Immediate extraction after quenching is the most important practical step. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)
• If the reaction stalls, ensure the vessel is properly sealed and that heating is adequate (100 °C internal temperature). Incomplete conversion can result from lower effective temperatures. (Org. Process Res. Dev. 2020)

### Reaction Type
Suzuki–Miyaura cross-coupling reaction.

### Mechanism Overview
Oxidative addition of the aryl halide to Pd(0) is typically rate-limiting. Transmetalation occurs from the boronic acid via an ate complex with hydroxide (generated from aqueous base). Reductive elimination forms the biaryl product. The aqueous/organic biphasic system facilitates transmetalation while keeping the catalyst in the organic phase.

### Solvent Notes
✓ Good: Toluene/EtOH/H₂O mixtures — Common for Suzuki couplings; toluene solubilizes organics, EtOH improves miscibility, aqueous phase provides the necessary base. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ Avoid: Fully anhydrous, aprotic solvents (e.g., pure toluene) — Inhibit formation of the reactive boronate species for transmetalation. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
• Pd(PPh₃)₄ is an effective pre-catalyst for couplings of aryl bromides/iodides under these mild conditions. (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Sodium carbonate is a mild, inexpensive base suitable for many Suzuki couplings. Stronger bases (e.g., K₃PO₄) can accelerate reactions but may increase side reactions for sensitive substrates. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
• Works well for electron-rich and electron-poor aryl bromides and iodides with arylboronic acids. (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Aryl chlorides generally require more active catalysts (e.g., Pd with bulky phosphine ligands). (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Sterically hindered ortho-substituted substrates may require longer reaction times or higher temperatures. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Functional Group Tolerance
✓ Tolerates: Nitro, ester, ketone, aldehyde, cyano groups under the mild conditions described. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ Incompatible: Not explicitly stated, but free carboxylic acids may require neutralization; very base-sensitive groups (e.g., esters with strong base) could be problematic.

### Critical Conditions
• Atmosphere: Reactions are typically run under nitrogen or argon to prevent oxidation of the Pd(0) catalyst. (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Temperature: Reflux (~110°C for toluene/EtOH) is common; some couplings proceed at lower temps (e.g., 80°C). (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Two-phase system must be vigorously stirred to ensure good mixing for transmetalation. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Side Reactions
• Protodeboronation: Decomposition of the boronic acid, especially for electron-rich or sterically hindered types, can be a side reaction. (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Homocoupling: Can occur if the catalyst system becomes oxidized; maintaining an inert atmosphere suppresses this. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Procedure Hints
• The boronic acid and base can be combined in the aqueous phase before adding the catalyst and aryl halide. (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Reaction progress can be monitored by TLC; the product is typically less polar than the boronic acid starting material. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Scale-up Notes
• Ensure vigorous mechanical stirring to maintain efficient mixing of the biphasic mixture at larger scale. (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Reflux conditions are easily scalable, but heat input/removal must be managed. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Analytical Notes
• TLC with hexanes/ethyl acetate is suitable for monitoring; the biaryl product is generally less polar than the aryl halide starting material. (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Crude product can be purified by recrystallization or chromatography. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips
• If reaction stalls, consider: 1) Adding more catalyst, 2) Increasing temperature, 3) Switching to a more active ligand system (e.g., SPhos) for difficult substrates. (Org. Synth. 2025)
• Yields can be optimized by ensuring the boronic acid is of good quality (dry, not overly oxidized). (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Reaction Type
Suzuki–Miyaura cross-coupling.


### Mechanism Overview
Oxidative addition of aryl halide to Pd(0), transmetalation of the organoboron reagent (activated by base to form a tetracoordinate borate), and reductive elimination. Rate-limiting step is typically oxidative addition for aryl chlorides; electron-rich phosphine ligands accelerate this step by stabilizing the Pd(0) catalyst and facilitating oxidative addition. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Solvent Notes
✓ Good: Toluene/ethanol/water mixtures — provide a biphasic environment that facilitates the aqueous base activation of the boronic acid while allowing solubility for organic substrates and catalyst. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ Good: 1,4-Dioxane/water — common for Suzuki couplings, offers good substrate solubility and miscibility with aqueous base. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes
✓ SPhos or XPhos ligands are highly effective for coupling aryl chlorides, which are more challenging than bromides or iodides. They are bulky, electron-rich dialkylbiarylphosphines that prevent Pd(0) aggregation and promote oxidative addition. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ Pd catalysts: Pd(OAc)₂ or Pd₂(dba)₃ are common precatalysts used with these ligands in situ. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ Base: K₃PO₄ is a common, weakly coordinating phosphate base that effectively activates boronic acids/esters for transmetalation without promoting excessive protodeboronation. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Substrate Scope and Limitations
✓ Works well with electron-rich, neutral, and moderately electron-deficient aryl/heteroaryl chlorides when using SPhos/XPhos. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ Arylboronic acids and esters (MIDA, BPin) are suitable coupling partners. Pinacol esters can offer improved stability against protodeboronation. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ Highly electron-deficient aryl chlorides (e.g., nitro-substituted) may react too slowly or require alternative conditions. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ Sterically hindered substrates (ortho-substituted on both partners) may give low yields. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Functional Group Tolerance
✓ Tolerates: ester, nitrile, ketone, alkyl chloride, silyl ether. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ Incompatible: Strongly coordinating groups (e.g., free thiols, some aliphatic amines) may poison the catalyst. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✗ Reducible groups like aldehydes or nitroso compounds may not be tolerated under these reducing conditions. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Critical Conditions
✓ Temperature: Reflux (~90–110°C) is typically required for aryl chlorides. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ Atmosphere: Reactions should be run under an inert atmosphere (N₂ or Ar) to prevent oxidation of the phosphine ligand and Pd(0) catalyst. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ Addition Order: Catalyst, ligand, and base are typically added to the substrate mixture before heating to form the active catalytic species. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Side Reactions
✓ Protodeboronation: Degradation of the boronic acid reagent, especially for electron-rich or hindered substrates. Using a boronic ester (e.g., BPin) or ensuring careful control of base and temperature can minimize this. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ Homocoupling: Formation of biaryl from the aryl halide (or boronic acid) can occur if the catalyst is deactivated or if oxidative addition is slow relative to other pathways. Using fresh catalyst, proper degassing, and correct ligand loading suppresses this. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ Reduction: The aryl halide may be reduced to the arene (dehalogenation) via β-hydride elimination from the solvent or ligand. Proper choice of ligand and solvent minimizes this. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Procedure Hints
✓ Use a Schlenk line or glovebox techniques for setting up reactions under inert atmosphere. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ The reaction mixture typically turns dark brown/black upon heating, indicating formation of the active Pd(0) catalyst species. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ For solids, mix all reagents thoroughly before adding solvent to ensure uniform catalyst distribution. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Scale-up Notes
✓ Concentration can often be increased (e.g., to 0.2–0.5 M) without significant detriment, improving throughput. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ Heat transfer and efficient stirring become more critical to avoid hot spots and ensure uniform reaction progress. (Org. Synth. 2025)
✓ Exotherm is generally not a major concern for this type of coupling. (Org. Synth. 2025)

### Analytical Notes
✓ Reaction progress is best monitored by TLC (visualized by UV or CAM stain) or by LCMS for the disappearance of the aryl halide starting material. (Org. Synth. 2025)
