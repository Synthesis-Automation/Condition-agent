### Reaction Type

Photoexcited nitroarenes as oxygen-atom transfer reagents for the anaerobic oxidative cleavage of alkenes.

### Mechanism Overview

* Nitroarenes serve as stoichiometric oxygen atom donors via photoexcitation at 390 nm, generating an excited-state nitrene-oxide-like species that transfers an oxygen atom to the alkene, forming an epoxide intermediate. Subsequent C–C bond cleavage and rearrangement yields carbonyl products. This avoids the use of gaseous oxidants like O₂ or O₃. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 114-127; *   The reaction is performed under anaerobic conditions to prevent overoxidation of sensitive substrates and side reactions from photooxidation by atmospheric oxygen, which is a common problem in photocatalytic aerobic cleavage systems. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 114-127;

### Solvent Notes

* **Acetonitrile** — Used as the solvent; it dissolves both nitroarenes and alkene substrates well, is transparent to the 390 nm light used for excitation, and is sufficiently polar to stabilize polar intermediates. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 114-127;

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

* **Nitroarenes** — Electron-deficient nitroarenes (e.g., 4-nitrobenzonitrile) are effective as stoichiometric oxidants. Their strong absorption at ~390 nm facilitates photoexcitation, and the electron-withdrawing group stabilizes the reduced byproduct. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 114-127; *   **No metal catalyst** — This is a metal-free oxidative cleavage method, avoiding issues with metal contamination, cost, and toxicity associated with traditional ozonolysis or metal-catalyzed methods. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 114-127;

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

* **Works well for:** Styrene derivatives (e.g., 1,1-diphenylethene), aliphatic terminal alkenes, and internal alkenes. The method is complementary to ozonolysis as it tolerates oxidatively sensitive functional groups. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 114-127; *   **Limitations:** The Discussion notes that transition-metal methods often have narrow applicability ranges, and enzymatic catalysis faces specificity and rate challenges. * **✓ Tolerates oxidatively sensitive groups** — The protocol explicitly demonstrates tolerance to functional groups that would be problematic with ozonolysis or aerobic photocatalytic cleavage, allowing for complementary substrate scope. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 114-127;

### Critical Conditions

* **Atmosphere** — Strictly anaerobic conditions (N₂ atmosphere) are essential to prevent overoxidation and competing side reactions with atmospheric oxygen. A thorough nitrogen purge before reaction initiation is required. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 114-127; ***Light source** — 390 nm LED lamps are critical for exciting the nitroarene. The reaction flask must be positioned close (~3 cm) to the light sources for effective irradiation. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 114-127;*   **Temperature control** — Irradiation generates heat; active cooling (e.g., a strong flow of N₂ directed at the flask or fans) is necessary to maintain a temperature of ~30-35°C and prevent thermal degradation or side reactions. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 114-127;

### Side Reactions

* **Overoxidation** — A key advantage of this anaerobic method is the suppression of overoxidation to carboxylic acids, a common side reaction in aerobic oxidative cleavage systems. (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 114-127;
