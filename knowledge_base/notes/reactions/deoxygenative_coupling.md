### Reaction Type

Deoxygenative allylic C–H amination/coupling to form meta-substituted benzenes via molybdenum catalysis. Key variant: intermolecular coupling of an ynone with an allylic amine.

### Mechanism Overview

• The key transformation involves molybdenum-catalyzed deoxygenative coupling. The catalytically active species is generated in situ by heating Mo(CO)₆ with a quinone (3,5-di-tert-butyl-1,2-benzoquinone) in toluene at 160 °C, forming a dark red-purple homogeneous solution (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C, Note 22, Note 23).
• The proposed mechanism involves the allylic amine reacting with the activated molybdenum catalyst, generating methylamine as a byproduct. The ynone is used in excess (2.4 equiv) to capture this liberated methylamine and prevent side reactions (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C, Note 24).
• The phosphine ligand (DPPO) is crucial for the reaction, likely stabilizing the molybdenum catalyst and influencing selectivity. Its synthesis requires strict anhydrous/anaerobic conditions to prevent oxidation of the diphenylphosphide anion and the final product (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step A, Note 8).

### Solvent Notes

✓ **Dry, degassed toluene** — Used as the solvent for the key molybdenum-catalyzed coupling. It withstands high temperature (160 °C) and is suitable for generating the active Mo catalyst (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C).
✓ **Dry THF** — Used for the generation of lithium diphenylphosphide in DPPO synthesis. It solvates the organolithium reagent effectively at low temperature (-78 °C) (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step A).
✓ **Triethylamine/THF mixture** — Serves as both solvent and base for the Sonogashira-type ynone synthesis, scavenging HCl generated from the acid chloride (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step B).
✗ **Wet or oxygenated solvents** — Must be avoided, especially for phosphine ligand synthesis and molybdenum catalyst generation. Solvents should be distilled from appropriate drying agents (Na for THF, CaH₂ for Et₃N, Na for toluene) and transferred under inert atmosphere (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Note 4).

### Reagent and Catalyst Notes

• **Mo(CO)₆** — Serves as a pre-catalyst. Must be handled with care due to toxicity and volatility. The active catalyst is generated by thermolysis with a quinone co-catalyst at 160 °C (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C, Note 22).
• **3,5-Di-tert-butyl-1,2-benzoquinone** — Required as a co-catalyst/ligand with Mo(CO)₆ to generate the active species. Reaction color change (to dark red-purple upon heating) is a key visual indicator of catalyst activation (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C, Note 23).
• **DPPO Ligand (1,8-bis(diphenylphosphino)octane)** — A bidentate phosphine ligand critical for the molybdenum-catalyzed coupling. It is air-sensitive; solutions oxidize quickly, so extraction and concentration should be performed rapidly (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step A, Note 8).
• **n-BuLi** — Must be fresh and of known concentration. The checker found that a 2.0 M solution in cyclohexane did not work, while a 2.5 M solution in hexane was successful, indicating solvent and concentration sensitivity for the phosphide formation (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Note 5).
• **Pd(PPh₃)₂Cl₂/CuI** — Standard Sonogashira catalyst system for ynone synthesis. Works reliably with acid chlorides and terminal alkynes in amine solvent (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step B).

### Substrate Scope and Limitations

• **Ynone Component**: Aryl-substituted ynones (from acid chlorides and phenylacetylene) are demonstrated. The presence of the aryl group on the alkyne is likely important for stabilizing the intermediate or preventing side reactions (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step B).
• **Allylic Amine Component**: The procedure uses N-allylmethylamine. The reaction liberates methylamine, which must be captured by excess ynone. Other allylic amines may work but could generate different volatile amines that need trapping (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C, Note 24).
• **Excess Ynone**: Using the ynone in 2.4 equiv excess is proposed to be necessary to trap the methylamine byproduct and drive the reaction forward (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C, Note 24).

### Functional Group Tolerance

✓ **Tolerates**: Aryl chlorides/bromides (present in DPPO synthesis, but not under Mo catalysis conditions), ketones (ynone), aryl methyl groups (p-tolyl).
✗ **Incompatible (under Mo catalysis conditions)**: Not explicitly tested, but the high temperature (160 °C) and Lewis acidic/basic molybdenum catalyst likely preclude many base- or acid-sensitive groups. Free amines (like the generated methylamine) must be scavenged.
⚠ **Conditionally Tolerated**: The Sonogashira step tolerates acid chlorides and terminal alkynes but requires anhydrous conditions and a base like triethylamine (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step B).

### Critical Conditions

• **Temperature**: The molybdenum catalyst activation requires heating to 160 °C for 15 minutes. The subsequent coupling also runs at 160 °C for 48 hours (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C).
• **Atmosphere**: Strictly anhydrous and oxygen-free conditions (N₂ or Ar) are essential for all steps: phosphine ligand synthesis, ynone formation, and molybdenum-catalyzed coupling. Use of Schlenk techniques or glovebox is recommended (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step A, B, C, Note 3).
• **Order of Addition**: For DPPO synthesis, n-BuLi is added to cold THF before diphenylphosphine to generate lithium diphenylphosphide. The dibromooctane is then added to this anion at -78 °C (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step A).
• **Catalyst Pre-activation**: The Mo(CO)₆ and quinone must be pre-mixed and heated in toluene separately to generate the active catalyst before adding the other reagents (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C).

### Side Reactions

• **Oxidation of Phosphines**: The diphenylphosphine reagent and the DPPO product are highly air-sensitive. Exposure to air leads to phosphine oxide formation, deactivating the ligand (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step A, Note 8).
• **Homocoupling or Oligomerization of Ynone**: Using excess ynone (2.4 equiv) might lead to its dimerization or other side reactions if the amine component is limiting or if catalyst is not active. This is mitigated by the designed trapping of methylamine (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C, Note 24).
• **Incomplete Catalyst Activation**: If the Mo(CO)₆/quinone mixture is not heated sufficiently (160 °C, 15 min), the catalyst may not form, leading to low conversion. The color change to dark red-purple is an important indicator (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C).

### Procedure Hints

• **Visual Cues**: The Mo(CO)₆/quinone solution turns dark red-purple upon heating to 160 °C, indicating active catalyst formation. The final reaction mixture after 48 hours is a different color/composition (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C, Figure 4).
• **Atmosphere**: Perform all operations under positive nitrogen pressure using syringe techniques or in a glovebox. Evacuate and backfill Schlenk flasks three times (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Note 3).
• **Ligand Handling**: Work quickly with DPPO solutions during extraction and concentration to minimize air exposure (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step A, Note 8).
• **Safety**: When using sealed vessels (e.g., Schlenk tubes) for the high-temperature (160 °C) Mo-catalyzed reaction, a protective shield is recommended due to the risk of explosion from pressure build-up (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Note 21).

### Scale-up Notes

• **Heat Transfer**: The high-temperature (160 °C) step requires efficient heating and stirring, especially in larger vessels. The original authors used Schlenk tubes, while checkers used an EasyMax reactor with overhead stirring (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C, Note 21).
• **Mixing**: For the molybdenum-catalyzed step, efficient mixing is crucial due to potential heterogeneity. Overhead stirring (as in the EasyMax) is preferable to magnetic stirring at larger scales (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C).
• **Catalyst Pre-activation Volume**: When scaling, maintain the concentration of the Mo/quinone pre-activation step (approx. 1.2 mmol in 20 mL toluene) to ensure complete catalyst formation before transfer to the main reaction vessel (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C).

### Analytical Notes

• **NMR for Purity**: Quantitative ¹H NMR (qNMR) with an internal standard (1,3,5-trimethoxybenzene) is used to confirm >99% purity of final products and intermediates like DPPO (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step A, Note 11; Step B, Note 20; Step C, Note 28).
• **³¹P NMR for Ligand**: Characterization of the air-sensitive DPPO ligand includes ³¹P NMR, a critical tool for confirming the absence of phosphine oxides (δ = -16.1 ppm for DPPO) (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Note 11).
• **TLC/Chromatography**: For the ynone product **1**, chromatography on silica gel with petroleum ether/EtOAc (20:1) is effective, but an impurity may co-elute in later fractions, potentially requiring a second purification (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Note 17).

### Yield / Troubleshooting Tips

• **DPPO Synthesis Yield (91%)**: High yield depends on strict exclusion of air and moisture during the lithiation and alkylation steps. Use fresh, concentrated n-BuLi (in hexanes, not cyclohexane) (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step A, Note 5, Note 10).
• **Ynone Synthesis Yield (93%)**: The Sonogashira reaction is reliable. Post-reaction, adding EtOAc to the concentrated mixture precipitates byproducts (likely amine salts), simplifying filtration before chromatography (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step B, Note 16).
• **Final Coupling Yield (74%)**: Ensure the molybdenum catalyst is fully pre-activated (observe color change) and that the reaction is kept strictly anaerobic at 160 °C for the full 48 hours. Using excess ynone (2.4 equiv) is crucial for yield (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C, Note 24, Note 27).
• **Purification of Final Product**: For the terphenyl product **3**, dry-loading the crude mixture onto silica gel with CH₂Cl₂ before chromatography with pure petroleum ether provides good separation (Org. Synth. 2025, 102, 381-397; Step C, Note 26).
